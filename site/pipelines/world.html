Pipeline

Worlds

Build Roblox levels and maps from JSON blueprints — terrain shaped by rivers and real elevation data, scanned CC0 materials and meshes, roads laid first, playability proven by numbers, then walked in Studio. One world pushed to impressive beats five mediocre ones.

Tier 1+ for Blender look stations 1 approval (direction) 10 / 30 / 60 min clock (complex by default)

At a glance

Makes
Roblox levels and maps: battleground arenas, lobbies, obbies, towns, interiors, open terrain. Each world ships as a Rojo model in src/world (Workspace.KitWorld), with optional terrain, materials, and streaming.
Hardware
Tier 0 Blueprint building, gates, and the streaming check require only a CPU. Tier 1+ Blender 5.2 is required for eye-level look stations (1920×1080 renders beside reference stills) and material PBR preview.
Your approvals
One: the creative direction, before any building starts. You pick one of three rendered direction frames. After building, you give a verdict (0–10) which completes the pipeline.
Clock
Worlds are complex by default (30 min), or hero with explicit approval (60 min). Simple is 10 min. The clock starts when lune run parallax world builds any blueprint new or changed since the last build. Past the budget, no change rebuilds the world; show what exists and wait for your words.
Results land in
src/world (Workspace.KitWorld via Rojo), build/worlds/look/<Name>/pages/ (look stations), build/worlds/checks/<Name>.png (reachability map), build/worlds/web/<Name>.html (three.js viewer for art direction).

The flow in one line

direction (you pick) → refs → lessons → terrain → materials → meshes → dressing → check → look → Studio walk → verdict (you score 0–10)

The direction approval is the only hard lock before building. The check must reach 0 errors before the look stations are drawn.

The stages

  1. 0
    Direction you pickPick a creative direction from three rendered options before any building starts.

    Every original world gets a direction document first. The document specifies the intent, one central idea, six or more reference images from three or more different media (each with what to take and what to leave), three pillars, bans, and three directions that differ on at least two axes (rhythm, read, effort, mood).

    Each direction is rendered as a frame using the kit's real pieces (lune run parallax world frame <scene.frame.json>). The board shows all three frames side by side. You pick one. Building is refused until the direction document has your pick recorded in its ## Pick section.

    A color script (lune run parallax world colorscript) lays the world's places out in journey order: palette, value, and warmth. Neighbouring zones with no change of mood are flagged for rethinking.

    lune run parallax world direction <Piece> --new
lune run parallax world frame <scene.frame.json>   # render a direction frame
lune run parallax world board                        # show the three frames to you
lune run parallax world direction <Piece>            # checks the doc and your pick in ## Pick
lune run parallax world colorscript

    Produces: assets/worlds/direction/<Piece>.md (direction document) and its rendered frames.

  2. 1
    ReferencesMeasure six or more real stills to build a world identity from numbers.

    At least six real reference stills are measured by script: horizon line placement, value structure (how light, mid, and dark values are distributed), atmospheric haze, palette, and landmark placement. The measurements produce a REFS.md file that distils the world's visual identity as numbers.

    --vs <render.png> compares a render against the references: one difference and one edit, no score.

    References must come from real sources: game screenshots, photographs, film stills. Words alone make generic maps.

    lune run parallax world refs <Name>
lune run parallax world refs <Name> --vs <render.png>

    Produces: assets/worlds/refs/<Name>/ (reference stills), REFS.md (measured identity).

  3. 1b
    LessonsAnswer the vault's ranked lessons for this world before anything is built.

    Before building starts, the vault's lessons are ranked for this specific world (by its blueprint words, brief, and direction) and shown as a sheet. Up to five answer lessons must be applied (how) or skipped (with a reason of at least six words) before any build runs.

    Past house layouts (*.layout.json) are offered as examples for town and interior worlds — examples to draw ideas from, never templates to copy.

    Once answered, the applied lessons are recorded on the world. Your future verdict credits or flags each one, so the pipeline knows which lessons actually helped and which were noise.

    lune run parallax world lessons <Name>      # ranked sheet + answer prompt
lune run parallax world lessons <Name> --show  # re-check answers

    Produces: assets/worlds/<Name>.lessons.md.

  4. 2
    TerrainShape land by rivers and real hydrology; paint roles by climate and slope.

    Land is never shaped by noise and thermal erosion alone — that produces talus-angle cones that read as a wall. Instead, the terrain generator uses a stream-power incision pass to carve rivers into the heightmap, then thermal erosion, then droplet erosion, then a painter that assigns material roles (meadow, lush, rock, cliff, scree, sand, snow) by slope, altitude, and climate.

    Terrain generation flags:

    • --stream: stream-power river incision, so rivers carve the land. After terrain generation, run lune run parallax world rivers <Name> once: D8 drainage over the stored heights paints the cells where the most water gathers (the top 1.2%) as Water.
    • --thermal: thermal erosion (talus transport).
    • --erosion: droplet erosion (simulated rainfall).
    • Real elevation: lune run parallax world dem <Name> --bbox lon0,lat0,lon1,lat1 fetches USGS 3DEP data (US only, public domain) for a terrain design's "dem" base. A ~3 km box gives roughly 2.9 m per stud on a 1024-stud map. Refine real land lightly; the river pass is tuned for noise and over-carves real terrain.
    • --climate: a climate model (wind bearing, rainfall, sea temperature, lapse rate, north-face shade) determines the soil on each slope: lush on windward faces, bare behind ridges, snow above the snowline.

    Keep masks and an anchor (the lake bed anchor prevents the minimum height from moving during erosion) are part of the terrain spec. Land is designed with shapes (rim, plateau, basin, hill, ridge, ramp, dunes, ring) plus a domain warp, then eroded. Stamps (crater, mesa, cone, ridge, valley, mound, pit) are placed on the finished land after erosion.

    Always look at the heightmap relief and the paint before moving to materials. The heightmap samples are embedded in the world model so the game, the check, and the look all read the same heights.

    lune run parallax world terrain <Name> --stream --thermal --erosion
lune run parallax world rivers <Name>   # paint river cells as Water
lune run parallax world dem <Name> --bbox lon0,lat0,lon1,lat1  # real elevation (US only)

    Produces: assets/worlds/terrain/<Name>.hm.json (heightmap with painted roles), <Name>.hm.rivers.png (river map to inspect).

  5. 3
    MaterialsAssign scanned CC0 materials from ambientCG to each painter role.

    Each terrain role (meadow, cliff, rock, sand, etc.) is assigned a scanned surface from ambientCG at 1K resolution (CC0, vetted in VETLOG.md). These become MaterialVariants in src/materials/<World>.model.json. The game's Rojo project maps MaterialService.KitWorldMaterials to that folder. At runtime, a game script calls MaterialService:SetBaseMaterialOverride for each material.

    Stylised texture packs are never used in a realistic world. The goal is scanned PBR surfaces that look right in Roblox's lighting with no post-processing.

    lune run parallax materials --fetch <ids> --res 1K  # fetch scanned materials

    Produces: build/terrainmaps/ (color/normal/roughness PNGs), src/materials/<World>.model.json after upload.

  6. 4
    MeshesImport CC0 scanned rocks, cliffs, and trees; keep the total part count under 10,000.

    Scanned rock, cliff, and boulder meshes from Poly Haven (CC0, vetted in VETLOG.md) are imported via an import spec (assets/worlds/imports/<set>.json). Each mesh is split at 20,000 triangles or fewer. Textures are downsized to 1024 px. The total part count after import must stay under 10,000 (error threshold).

    Z-fighting between imported meshes is a warning. Meshes must not float above the terrain or bury themselves below it.

    Trees are grown by an L-system grammar (broadleaf, conifer, dead, shrub). A recipe says the grammar, the seed, the leaf card size and density. The pipeline grows it and writes the mesh. A tree already uploaded into the game by its name in the project's ids file is not asked for again.

    lune run parallax world import                    # import CC0 scanned meshes
lune run parallax world import <set>              # a specific import set

    Produces: mesh models in src/world, import records in assets/worlds/imports/.

  7. 5
    DressingLay roads first; set-dress everything else by story.

    Roads are always placed before scatter and cover. Scatter and cover rules keep off roads automatically. Set dressing follows story logic: what does a player see first, what draws them forward, what breaks up the silhouette.

    A town is planned before it is typed. lune run parallax world town <Name> draws the town plan from above: a square entered at its corners, streets that end on house fronts, terraced lots on a 4-stud grid, and one landmark on the main gate's line of sight. Read this plan before dressing anything. lune run parallax world town <Name> --kit then writes one house mesh recipe per house type. Twelve or more building steps typed individually is a build error — use a town step instead.

    Scatter generators (forest, scatter, cover) place objects by density and role. Each has keep masks to clear roads, spawn pads, and objective areas.

    Breakable objects (crates, barrels, walls) are placed with the breakable generator step. They connect to KitBreakable and KitHealth at runtime.

    lune run parallax world town <Name>         # draw town plan
lune run parallax world town <Name> --kit   # write house mesh recipes
lune run parallax world meshes --set town_<World>  # build house meshes

    Produces: updated blueprint JSON, town plan image (LOOK: line in every town build), house mesh recipes in assets/worlds/.

  8. 6
    CheckProve playability by numbers: reachability, traps, spawn fairness, camera clearance, knockback room, sightlines.

    The check gate must reach 0 errors before the look stations are drawn. It runs a full playability proof by numbers:

    • R6 reachability: voxelizes the world (1-stud cells; 2-stud cells on maps larger than 400,000 square studs) and walks every reachable floor using the R6 real jump arc (v = sqrt(2gJ)). Narrow areas that the coarse grid misses are rechecked at 1-stud cells. A spawn that cannot reach another spawn or an objective is an error; a walkable area no one can reach is a warning.
    • Traps: a reachable place from which no spawn can be reached again (a pit you fall into for good), 12 cells or more, is an error. A gap narrower than the voxel body-width that still holds a whole voxel can read as passable — this is a known limit of the voxel check.
    • Spawn fairness (a warning): the path distance from each spawn to the objectives, or to the floor's centre, must not differ by more than 15%.
    • Camera clearance (a warning): a third-person camera 12 studs back must not hit a wall within 4 studs over more than 20% of the reachable floor.
    • Knockback room (a warning, battlegrounds): the share of floor with 30 studs of open room to the nearest wall.
    • Sightlines and exposure: how much of the map sees each spot, drawn with a sightline histogram; an exposure imbalance between spawns is a warning.
    • Landmark guidance: each landmark must be visible from at least 70% of road samples. The longest blind stretch of road is named.
    • Pacing: no more than 60 seconds of road travel (at the declared player speed) between points of interest (objectives, landmarks, spawns).

    Look lint (floating props, buried props, trees through buildings, rocks or trunks on roads) is also cleared before the look stage.

    lune run parallax world check <Name>   # 0 errors required

    Produces: build/worlds/checks/<Name>.png (reachability map, coloured by zone), a plain-text report of every error and warning.

  9. 7
    Look stationsRender every station at 1920×1080 beside its reference still; one difference and one edit per station.

    Every look station is rendered at 1920×1080 in Blender. Each page shows the reference still above the render at the same framing. You open every page individually — not a contact sheet, not a thumbnail grid. For each station: one difference from the reference and one edit to make.

    Stations cover: each spawn (from behind the R6 rig), every view declared in the blueprint, back from each objective, and from above. A refs/<Name>/stations.json file says which reference still maps to which station and how to crop it.

    The web viewer (lune run parallax world web <Name>) is an art-direction tool, not a proof. It renders in a browser with a three.js shader (terrain layers, procedural materials, water, sky, height fog, sun shadows, HDR bloom, light shafts, grass, particles). Use it to explore the world's look and colour script. Spatial proof stays in the check gate and the Studio walk.

    The look command ends with LOOK: <folder> lines. A changed world's look is re-rendered automatically at the end of lune run parallax world.

    lune run parallax world look <Name>   # eye-level look stations
lune run parallax world web <Name>    # three.js web viewer (art direction)
lune run parallax world shots <Name>  # headless contact sheet (~1 min)

    Produces: build/worlds/look/<Name>/pages/ (one HTML page per station, reference above, render below), build/worlds/web/<Name>.html.

  10. 8
    Studio walkWalk the world in Roblox Studio's own lighting; check 0 console errors; get a verdict.

    Blender's lighting is a stand-in. The Studio walk is the final proof. The kit makes a scratch place (the world alone; nothing saved or uploaded), and capture.luau. You open the scratch place in your own Studio window, start lune run parallax world studio <Name> --capture in the background, then run the capture script once through the Studio MCP (using that window's Studio ID, never another place's). This produces three-pane station images: reference, Blender render, and Studio capture side by side.

    Then rojo serve games/<Name>/default.project.json serves the full project. The Studio MCP walks every station and asserts 0 console errors. You walk the world yourself, then give a verdict.

    lune run parallax world studio <Name>
lune run parallax world studio <Name> --capture  # run in background after opening place
# in Studio: run capture.luau once via MCP
rojo serve games/<Name>/default.project.json
lune run parallax verdict world <Name> 8 "dark tone reads clearly from the spawn"

    Produces: three-pane station pages in build/worlds/studio/<Name>/.

Inputs and outputs

  • assets/worlds/<Name>.json: blueprint — generator steps, objectives, checks config, streaming declaration, lighting preset, terrain ops.
  • assets/worlds/<Name>.brief.md: free-text brief.
  • assets/worlds/terrain/<Name>.hm.json: heightmap with painted roles, produced by the terrain command.
  • assets/worlds/refs/<Name>/: reference stills for look stations.
  • assets/worlds/imports/<set>.json: CC0 mesh import lists (Poly Haven rocks, cliffs).
  • refs/<Name>/stations.json: which reference still maps to which look station, and how to crop it.
  • Direction document and rendered frames from stage 0.
  • build/worlds/<Name>.*: world model files.
  • build/worlds/checks/<Name>.png: reachability map (coloured by zone, unreachable areas marked).
  • build/worlds/look/<Name>/pages/: one HTML page per look station (reference above, Blender render below, labelled).
  • build/worlds/web/<Name>.html: three.js web viewer for art direction.
  • build/worlds/studio/<Name>/: three-pane station pages (reference, Blender, Studio).
  • src/world: Workspace.KitWorld via Rojo.
  • src/materials/<World>.model.json: MaterialVariant models.

The world loads into Workspace.KitWorld through Rojo. At runtime:

-- streaming: tell the kit where to stream around the player
local WorldStream = require(ReplicatedStorage.kit.WorldStream)
WorldStream.arrive(player, "Keep gate")  -- requests Roblox streaming before moving the character
WorldStream.ahead(player, "Bridge")      -- requests without moving

-- breakables:
-- KitBreakable and KitHealth components are on breakable parts automatically

-- animated grass:
-- decoration = true in the blueprint enables Roblox animated Grass terrain

The game plays the same heightmap that the check and the look used: heightmap samples are stored in KitTerrain<i>_<k> ModuleScript chunks inside the world model and assembled at runtime by the kit's Environment. Rojo cannot carry terrain or lighting effects directly.

How it learns

The pipeline improves from every world you score and every lesson you answer.

  • Vault lessons: the lessons applied to a world are recorded on it, and your verdict credits or flags each one.
  • Past house layouts: for town and interior worlds, past layouts are offered as examples when answering lessons. The pipeline never copies a layout; it uses them as a range of what has worked.
  • Golden set: a verdict of 7 or above adds the world to the golden set.
  • Graveyard: a verdict of 3 or below records the world's route in the graveyard. Failed routes (five showcase worlds instead of one, terrain from noise and thermal erosion alone, backdrop ranges of long straight ridges, primitive-part trees, buildings and rocks) are listed and refused without your word.
  • Vault distillation: after enough new verdicts, the kit distils evidence into live rules for the world vault.

Known limits

Known limit

Coloured light on water turns muddy. Light water by intensity only; keep the hue neutral.

Known limit

Yards, markets, and lamps are checked for presence, not for quality. The check gate passes a yard that exists but reads poorly.

Known limit

The voxel reachability check uses 1-stud cells (2 studs on maps larger than 400,000 square studs). A gap narrower than the voxel body-width that still holds a whole voxel can read as passable. Narrow stairs and doorways that the coarse grid misses are rechecked at 1-stud cells and listed as fine-rechecked in the stats.

Known limit

Roblox's StreamAroundAsync returns nothing on a timeout and makes no guarantee. A 5-second streaming request can come back "late". The kit's WorldStream.arrive handles this by requesting before moving the character, but a late response still means the character arrives into an unloaded area.

Known limit

The three.js web viewer and the Blender look stations do not draw SDF caves or overhangs — land the SDF op adds is missing there, and cave openings look solid. The voxel check and the Studio walk are the only proofs of SDF playability.

Known limit

The weather cycle system ("weather": {"cycle": [...]}) has been written and passes its spec tests, but has not been seen to run in a live Roblox session.

Known limit

Town playability is proved by the check gate and the Studio walk, not by the town plan generator alone. The generator lays out a geometrically valid town; playability (whether a player can reach every building, whether the streets flow well) is confirmed by the check's reachability pass.
