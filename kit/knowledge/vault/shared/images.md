# Images and looking (expensive; and the only honest judge of art)

- The four art lanes (3d, vfx, anim, world): NO hourly cap (the owner, 2026-11-11: "Yes, lift it"; card 3h44). The
  picture is the product and a thumbnail cannot judge it: the 2026-11-10 Gjallarhorn was judged from 512 x 384 views
  (the wolf about 40 px wide) and its score wandered 62-68 for eleven rounds; an animation beat sheet gave each beat
  about 150 x 85 px. Look at EVERY build / round, at the size the owner judges at: zoom into each hero feature at 1:1
  (a crop, not the whole sheet) with the reference beside it at the same framing; for motion, frames large enough to
  read the pose or the shape. A look that cannot resolve the feature being judged is not a look.
- Every other lane (ui, sfx, code, qa, kit): images cost ~350-1,600 tokens each and ride in context forever: ≤ 10
  per hour across a session AND its agents (department missions: ≤ 3 per hour for yourself). Crop tight; compose
  contact sheets; sample pixels by script when a number will do (a plate's mean under ~90/255 reads as steel; a lit
  cell's max channel under 245).
- In EVERY lane a picture rides in context forever and a bloated session dies: crop to the feature being judged,
  never re-open the same picture, and hand a bulk comparison (many crops, many candidates) to a fresh subagent that
  answers in words.
- Never report a model, a screen or an effect on a score (yours or an agent's): open the render / sheet / capture
  first. A score is gameable and has been gamed (the score-chased crate; the crispness tournament).
- Look at EVERY milestone: `build/renders/<name>.png` beside the reference photo; `build/ui/<Screen>.png` at phone /
  tablet / desktop + the reel; `build/vfx/booth/<name>_all.png`; `kit anim look --rig R6` sheets with the fidelity
  line. A model that never looks at its sheet ships what the gates allow.
- Android / device UI (other projects): never screenshots; uiautomator XML dumps + logcat.
- Judges read 50% sheets as screen pixels: give them 1:1 crops with rulers; fix the packet when every judge repeats
  the same packet complaint.
