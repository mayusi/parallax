# parallax-3d

**One planned pass through the stages, never a loop of rebuilds.** Every tool below exists to get a model RIGHT the
first time: all the references before any shape, a generated 3D draft before any code, every part made by a STACK
of modules (never one alone), proportions matched by the kit (`kit fit`), the surface from measured data, and a LOOK at the render
beside the reference pictures at the end of every stage. The stages are the iterations: blockout → primary forms →
secondary → detail → surface → polish happen INSIDE them, each checked in seconds (`kit cad check`, `kit watch
--geo`) — a full build is a stage's end, not a way to find out what to do next. (The Glock 17 of 2026-10-08 took 28
builds and ~7 hours of reference → trace → CAD → gates, with one photo traced and none of the generators, vision
models or surface tools used: the kit's floor said 85, fresh judges 49. That loop is what this page replaces.)

## Start here: `kit make` (the kit runs the pipeline, not you)

```
lune run kit make <name> "<the object>" --tier prop|complex|hero [--private] [--photos | --concept] [--fresh]
```
ONE command makes the model, inside the owner's hard budget — **10 minutes for a prop, 30 complex, 60 a hero**. It
runs every stage below by itself: the object's category and graded sizes (its dossier if it has one); its VIEWS —
a real named object's photos (the dossier's turntable, else `refs find` + `refs rank`; a name with a number or a
weapon is "real"), a generic one's CONCEPT sheet (`vision concept --sheet views`: PHOTOREAL front, left side, back of
ONE design — found photos of "a wooden chair" are many chairs, a museum's black-and-white one among them); each cut
out and traced (a real photo is measured against, a concept only REGISTERED for painting — never measured); a much
sharper dossier photo of a view (the maker's 8124 px Glock side) projected instead of the rig's frame, its side
checked by its outline (a label can be wrong: that "side-right" photo was the LEFT side) and its colour matched to the
rig's; reference models found and GRADED (torn surfaces, floating debris, a raw scan's jagged surface, proportions —
`kit/blender/model_quality.py`); base shapes from every generator the views allow (Hunyuan multi-view, Pixal3D,
TRELLIS.2 single, PartCrafter parts — a later one only if the paint and build still fit after it, each stopped at the
time left) and, from a real object's own traced side + front photos, its visual hull twice: boolean (make_hull: crisp)
and SCULPTED (make_sculpt_hull: always closed, edges rounded 1.2% of its length); every candidate CLEANED (welded, specks dropped, holes filled, faces outward, at most 300k faces; a hard
surface also denoised with its edges kept and its flat faces made flat: `rbx_denoise`) before a TOURNAMENT that
renders each from the views and scores outline, look and proportions (crispness is reported, never scored: it once
picked a crisp but warped Glock); the winner fitted per axis to graded sizes (`import_mesh(stretch=True)`), painted
(Hunyuan Paint — a painted mesh that comes back torn, over 5% open edges, is refused), given one finish by what it is
made of (`rough=`, `scan=`: the generator's metal / roughness guesses replaced) and a LIFE read off its words (`life=`:
"an old chest" heavy, "a new chair" new, else light — dirt in its crevices, worn edges: never factory-fresh), and its
views PROJECTED onto it with
their fine structure as relief (`a.project(..., relief=)`), one side view mirrored to the other; the build, the render
beside the views, and for a hero the judge packet.
Every module ends RAN or SKIPPED with a reason the code checked — the report (`build/make/<name>/report.md`) fails
on one that did neither.
- When a generated model looks WRONG (see-through, a pencil sketch, black cracks), measure its mesh before blaming
  textures: welded, a closed triangle mesh has about half as many vertices as faces and ~0 open edges
  (`make.glb_topology`). A flat-shaded glTF is a triangle soup; decimated as one, it shreds.
- A **hand-written hero / aaa build builds at its tier and must pass EVERY hero check, no waiver** (`rbx.Asset`;
  the owner, 2026-11-11, kit card 38a9: it replaced the 2026-10-09 refusal of every hero kit make did not make, and
  the KIT_HAND_BUILD switch): a judgement it declares off (`see_through=True`, `through=True`, `stage_gate=False`)
  fails it. A script with no `quality=` builds at its BRIEF's tier (`- quality: hero` in `<name>.brief.md`). His
  7+ / keep pieces whose script is unchanged since he judged it build as before (`board/grandfathered.json`, by
  sha1). A made model's hand fixes still go in `assets/models/<name>.override.py`, run on top of the made model with
  `a` and `rbx` in scope — never instead of it.
- After it: LOOK at `build/make/<name>/compare.png` beside the photo; a hero's judges get
  `build/judge/<name>/PROMPT.md`. A candidate made before from the same photos is reused (`--fresh` remakes it).
- The stages below are what it runs, and what an override reaches for.

### Rules of the pass
1. **All the data first.** Stage 1 gathers every picture, size and reference model the parts need — a fact found
   mid-build costs a rebuild (the Silverballer's half-cock hammer, the Glock's 7%-short scan). Ask the owner ONCE, up
   front, for what only they can get (the design's own pictures, close-ups).
2. **Never trust one source — and never a model's memory.** A real object's facts live in its DOSSIER (`kit
   dossier`), each graded from its sources: A the maker / a patent office / the owner, B two independent sites agree,
   C one source, D disagreement. A number a model typed counts only once a script found it in the cited source
   (`--quote`, the page's own words) or two reviewers (one blind) confirmed it. Pictures and models are measured by
   script against the A/B facts (the Glock's 7%-short scan and the Gen4's 204 mm both show there) and reviewed by two
   keys; a wrong reference is withdrawn the moment it shows, and another one found.
3. **A 3D draft before shape code.** A few minutes of GPU per generator (TRELLIS.2 ~4, Pixal3D ~6.5) — each run
   with the card otherwise IDLE, one at a time (a second GPU job spills into shared memory and runs 4-20x slower):
   do the CPU work — sizes, reference models, the parts plan — meanwhile. The 3D look-target, a proportion check
   against the reference models, and the shape itself for sculpted / organic parts (stage 2).
4. **Every part gets a STACK, never one module** — chosen in the brief's parts plan from the table below: its DATA
   (pictures of every side, a reference model's sections, a sourced size, learned bevels), its SHAPE (CAD, a loft
   from measured slices, a generated piece — often two together, with the draft as the look-target), its SURFACE (a
   measured finish, a scan, a pattern at the real pitch, engraving, projected detail) and its CHECKS. A gun made with
   CAD alone is a screwdriver on a nut: the CAD gives the exact shape, the rest gives the look.
5. **The kit matches proportions, not you:** a number the references should decide is `a.param()`, and `kit fit`
   moves it to the traced views (CMA-ES over --geo builds) — never nudged by hand build after build.
6. **The fast loops:** a CAD part in seconds with `kit cad check <name> --only <Part>` (no Blender); the whole
   model's checks with `kit watch <name> --geo` (a persistent Blender, no bake: seconds on a light asset, 2-7 minutes
   on a CAD-heavy hero — so iterate CAD parts alone and run --geo at milestones); a full `kit asset` only at a stage's
   end. `kit fit` runs one --geo build per evaluation: on a slow hero fit only the few proportions that matter
   (`--only a,b --evals 24`). The vision models WARM: `lune run kit vision serve` in its own terminal (or in the
   background) keeps them loaded — every `kit vision` edges / cutout / mask / depth / similar / differ / view call and
   every cut-out and rank goes through it (the kit's python models; measured: 10 calls 99.8 s
   cold -> 13.8 s warm, outputs pixel-identical; a cut-out ~0.6 s instead of ~11 s); a generator or a big vision model
   frees its GPU memory first by itself. Generate in the background while you write CAD and gather sizes / reference
   models (CPU work); GPU work — traces, cut-outs, other generators — waits until it finishes.
7. **LOOK at the end of every stage** — the render beside its reference pictures (`build/renders/<name>.png`,
   `kit vision differ <render> <photo>`), never the score: the score counts parts, triangles and materials.
8. **Time-box every fight.** A part that fails twice the same way (a fillet OpenCASCADE refuses, a join that loses
   material) changes approach: a simpler profile, its own body, another module in the part's stack.
9. **Judge once, fix once** (stage 6): fresh judges, one fix list, one round; another round only for what the look
   still shows wrong.

### Which stack for which part
Every part uses several modules — pick each part's stack here, adjust it to the part, write it in the parts plan.

| The part is ... | Data (stage 1) | Shape (stages 2-3) | Surface (stage 4) | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| machined / moulded, sizes known (a slide, frame, barrel, receiver) | photos of every side + the part's close-up (`kit refs find`, `kit trace --part-box / --part-text`); 2 reference models (`kit library inspect`, `kit refs measure`); sourced sizes (`kit refs dims`); bevels (`kit learned show`) | `a.cad` from `kit refs profiles` + the models' sections (`smooth_profile`, `fillet_each`); `a.param` + `kit fit`; the generated draft beside it as the look-target | `rbx.finish(...)` + `scan=` at the measured colour (`kit vision albedo`); edge wear; engraved markings; `a.project(detail=1.0)` for fine structure | `part_match`, `section_match`, `ref_sizes`, `a.learned_from`, `lines` |
| wrapping round (a grip, a stock, a shroud) | a reference model's slices (`kit refs measure`: levels); front + back photos; its texture's real pitch (`kit refs dims`) | an a.cad loft `loft_sections(from_levels(...))`; sculpted: its part of the draft (`kit gen parts`) | `pattern=` at the real `pattern_mm` + `a.zone` for the textured panels; `scan=` | `section_match`, `part_match` |
| a standard small part (screw, pin, spring, nut) | ISO rows (`kit refs dims find "iso 4762 m3"`); where it sits: an exploded / patent drawing (`kit refs patent`) | `cad_parts`: `screw`, `pin`, `roll_pin`, `spring`, `nut`, `clearance` | `rbx.finish(...)` (blued, black oxide, stainless) | `fastener_seat`, `fastener_support` |
| sculpted / organic / ornate (a head, a creature, a carved stock, foliage) | the biggest, cleanest pictures; chart views cut out (`kit vision cutout`); other sides to LOOK at (`kit vision angle`); depth (`kit vision depth`) | `a.sculpt` (clay in code: blends, strokes, creases, relief — the traced views as its guide); `kit gen views` / `kit gen` / `kit gen parts` → `a.import_mesh(..., crop=)` → `s.add_part` to repair and re-sculpt it; or a code form + `kit vision relief` → `a.displace` / `a.relief` | its generated textures, or `kit gen texture` → `a.texture_from`; `scan=` | `trace_match` per view, the look beside the photos |
| a whole static prop with good views | the views, cut out | `kit gen views` → a master, the parts that move cut out or code-built | its generated textures / `kit gen texture`; scans | `trace_match` per view |
| a marking (maker, model, serial, a logo) | its place and size measured on a close-up (`kit refs crop`, `rbx_trace.px`) | `a.cut(part, a.text(..., "engraving", depth=...))`; a logo: trace its close-up (`kit trace`) and cut the outline in | a printed / painted mark: `a.decal` | `marking_placement` |
| the finish and colour of EVERY part | `kit vision albedo` + `kit refs colour` on the most neutrally lit picture; wear and roughness by role (`kit learned show`) | — | `rbx.finish(...)` / `rbx.real(...)`, `scan=`, `wear=` / `grime=`; a look no material imitates: `kit gen texture` → `a.texture_from` | `colour_match`, `roughness_variation`, `pbr_values` |

## Stage 0 — Brief: what it is, how good, and how each part gets made
**Ask first.** `kit new asset <name>` writes `assets/models/<name>.brief.md`: ASK THE OWNER its questions before any
modelling (AskUserQuestion if your harness has it) — what it is, the END GOAL (master · game · showcase), the quality
and what "good" means to them, a QUALITY BAR (2-3 links / pictures of models at the level they want), the STYLE
(realistic PBR · stylised), the VIEW (first person · third person · world prop; how big on screen; which sides face
the camera), how much must WORK and the MOVING PARTS gameplay needs (only those), the EXTRAS it needs to work (a key
for its lock, the inside of a chest, a magazine), references, whose design it is (someone else's -> `private=True`),
and the HERO FEATURES: what players notice first, ranked, one line each with its ROUTE —
`1. wolf head | relief | the pommel's sculpted wolf | refs/side.png 410,120,470,170 | 30 mm`
(route: code · generated · relief · projected · procedural; the picture + its box + the size when a picture shows it).
Write their answers over each `<answer>`; every build warns `brief` until it is answered, reads it into
`facts.brief` (view, hero_features, quality_bar, style, moving_parts) and prints the hero features as a table
(`hero_features`: rank, route, picture, best px/mm). Too much and too little both waste the owner's time — the
brief is how you know which: the effort goes to the hero features, not to mechanisms nobody sees.
Hero / aaa builds warn `routes` for a hero feature with no route, or a sculpted / organic one built as `code`.
**The parts plan** (hero / aaa: `kit plan` asks for it) — under `- parts plan:` in the brief, every part you will
make, one numbered line each: `<part> | <shape modules> | <data> | <surface>` — its STACK from the table above,
e.g. `3. Slide | code: a.cad from the profiles + the draft as look-target | side + top traces, 2 models' sections,
186 mm (dims) | black nitride + scan, serrations, engraved marks, detail projection`. (The shape field names the
route words `kit plan` reads: code · generated · relief · projected · procedural — several where several are
used.) Deciding it here is what makes the pass ONE pass: no part starts with a stack that cannot reach the look, and
every part's data is gathered in stage 1 before it is needed.
**Declare the bar** in the script:
```python
a = rbx.Asset("marksman", ground=False, quality="aaa", ref_image="refs/marksman.png")
```
Tiers: **prop 55** (background props) · **hero 75** (anything players hold or look at) · **aaa 88** (showcase).
`lune run kit lessons 3d` · `lune run kit examples asset`. Get a REFERENCE image from the owner (or ask) and put it
in `refs/<name>.png`. Is there a template for the category? Use it.

## Stage 1 — Gather: every reference before any shape
**The dossier first — what is KNOWN about a real object, graded** (`lune run kit dossier ...`, kit/refs/dossier.py).
Compiled ONCE per real object and reused by every asset of it (`kit/knowledge/<slug>.json`; a private one in
`~/.cache/roblox-kit/knowledge/`); `kit plan` holds Gather until the owner approved it. From just a NAME:
`kit dossier new "<object>" --category <pistol | rifle | generic> --maker-domain <the maker's site>` prints the
category's research list (kit/knowledge/categories/) — follow it in order: the maker's own page for THIS variant, its
owner's manual (parts list with numbers, exploded drawing, field-strip order, a technical data table that names the
sibling variants' numbers too), its patent list (design patents draw clean orthographic views), its own pictures (a
360-degree turntable: one distance, one light — the best measuring pictures), then CC pictures and reference models.
A maker's turntable is also a MODEL: `kit refs hull <folder of its photos> --length-mm <the A length> --name <name>
--side-col <the side photo's column> --front left|right` finds the camera from the photos themselves, carves the
object's visual hull, scales it to the published length and MEASURES the rest (its height and widths check the other
published sizes), and renders the square top / front / side OUTLINES no camera takes (add them with `--kind
drawing`, the hull with `--kind model --measure refs/<name>.measure.json`) — outlines only, their widths upper bounds;
its rear outline is its front one flipped (the check calls a mirrored copy the same picture: add one). Views WITH
detail (a rear sight, a backstrap) come from a confirmed reference model: `kit refs measure <model> --name <n>
--length-mm <A length> --views all [--front min]` renders its shaded square side / top / front / rear at 6 px/mm
(`build/refs/<n>_<view>_view.png`; LOOK that the front points right first). A reference model with an accessory (a
weapon light) is measured without it: `kit refs measure ... --skip <word>` — else its length scales the gun wrong.
Then CUT the build against the model: `kit refs sections <name> --ref <library id> [--ref-hide <word>] [--flip]` —
cross-sections at the same stations behind the muzzle (dense at the nose), the reference red over ours blue, each
station MEASURED apart (p90 mm; the reference's shape our build lacks, ours it has not): a solid where the real part
is hollow, a box where it is round, a spring 4 mm too high — none of it shows from the side (the Glock's frame and
nose, 2026-10-10). Do it at the Shape stage for every hero part, before any surface work.
A PHOTO carries its camera's perspective (the Glock 17's 35 px/mm side photo came out 2.4% too tall, its dust cover
1.9 mm thin, its grip 1.6 mm long): lay its trace onto a CALIBRATED outline of the same view — the turntable hull's,
a confirmed model's square view — with `kit refs rectify <photo trace> --to <calibrated trace> --level <slide>` (one
projective map, found edge to edge, taken out of every outline, hole and part), then `kit refs profiles <name>_rect`
and build FROM the rectified profiles; a number measured by hand on the raw photo goes through the same map. A HERO
build is matched against EVERY traced view it has (`assets/models/<name>_*.trace.json`, found by name, IoU 0.9 — the
Glock build once had traces and no trace_match at all); a trace made from data found wrong is excluded with its reason:
`a.not_reference("<trace>", why="...")`; where a reference differs on purpose (a model's night sights, flattened
levers) a `dont_care=` box with the reason in a comment.
A size the maker publishes OVER a part (GLOCK's 34 mm width is over the slide stop levers, which a hull carves away
and a model flattens) is recorded beside it, measured ON the confirmed models WITHOUT that part: find where each
model's extremes are (a model's box is lever + magwell + tilt, not "the body"), `kit refs measure <model> --name <n>_cut
--cut <L0..L1>,<H0..H1>` (mm from the rear / from the bottom: the sizes without that box, recorded), then `fact --dim
"width without slide stop levers" --mm 31.8 --from <model id> --measure refs/<n>_cut.measure.json` — B at most (two
independent sites agreeing), the number checked by script, its MEANING by two keys (one blind). Named in the category's
`frame`, a view or model then passes against either meaning (and says so: "checked against ... measured ON the
reference models"). A row added wrong: `withdraw <object> row:<id> --why ..` (its adder or the owner; a reviewer rejects
instead). One model is ONE source however many pages or files carry it; a hull's OBJ is right-handed (right side +z).
Record as you go: `source --url .. --kind manual --fetch`, `fact --part slide --dim length --mm 186 --source s1
--quote "<the page's words around the number>"` (refused when the page does not say it), `part --no 1 --name Slide
--quote "1 Slide"`, `tell --text "no finger grooves" --vs "<sibling>" --quote ..` (what sets this variant apart),
`add --url/--file <picture> --page <where it came from> --scope measure-only|ship-ok --view side-left` (a private
asset may use the maker's photos as MEASURING references only — never shipped, never committed; the files live outside
the repo; the owner's own photo: `--scope owner --as owner --note "<when they gave it>"`),
`add --kind model --measure refs/<name>.measure.json`, `withdraw <id> --why ..` for one added wrong. `kit dossier
check` grades every fact, measures every picture (px/mm, proportions against the A/B facts, duplicates) and every
model's proportions, and scores what the category needs (views, close-ups, anatomy, tells, models). Then review in ONE
batch: `kit dossier review "<object>" --as <you>` (look at its contact sheet, answer each item: object, variant and
the tells you saw, stock, view, square-on, what it is good for), and a FRESH reviewer `--blind` (no claims, no earlier
verdicts) — an item counts once two different reviewers accepted it, one blind, one not its adder. `kit dossier sheet`
-> the reference sheet for the owner; on their yes, `kit dossier approve "<object>" --as owner --sheet <the id on the
sheet they saw> --note "<what they said>"` (stale the moment anything graded changes). A number typed from memory
never counts; a file path is no source; the owner's own measurement is `fact --source owner --as owner --note ..`. Then `kit pack <name> object "<object>" --length dossier` and
`kit pack <name> size <part.dim> dossier` take the graded sizes (A / B only). Web search ranks machine-written content
farms above the maker: `kit/knowledge/sources.json` blocks the ones found — add every new one a reviewer finds.
Everything below happens BEFORE the first shape: pictures of every side and every part (sharp), sizes with sources,
1-2+ reference MODELS measured and looked inside, the colours measured, each hero feature at >= 2 px/mm. Start the
stage-2 generators as soon as the first views are cut out — each when the GPU is idle (never beside SAM tracing),
the CPU work (sizes, reference models, the parts plan) meanwhile.
**The pack — every result in one place** (`lune run kit pack <name>`, `assets/models/<name>.pack.json`): it FINDS what
the asset has by the kit's own naming (traces and their mm profiles, the reference models measured / inspected under
its name or cited by its script, generated drafts, lighting-free colour pictures, fitted proportions) and KEEPS what
you choose: `kit pack <name> object "<what it is>" --length dims:<id> --category <kind>` (the real length every size
hangs on), `kit pack <name> size slide.length dims:<id>` (a size with its source; plain mm is said UNSOURCED),
`kit pack <name> colours --trace <name>_photo_side` (each traced part's MEDIAN colour, roughness and metalness inside
its own mask on `kit vision albedo`'s lighting-free picture — run that first), `kit pack <name> time <stage>
<seconds>`. The build reads it: `pk = a.pack()` → `pk.length_mm`, `pk.size("slide.length")`, `pk.profile("slide")`
/ `pk.extents("slide")` / `pk.outline()` / `pk.holes()` (mm, kit refs profiles' frame; the SIDE view's unless `view=`

or a search (`--fetch` runs `kit refs find "<object> <part> close up"`).
**Real objects: research → spec → trace → model (in that order).** Never model a real object from one photo.
1. References: a TRUE SIDE VIEW (the maker's product shot, a manual / patent drawing, a blueprint — a 3/4 photo traces
   wrong everywhere), plus the manual / exploded view for what is INSIDE and how it works. Log each in `refs/SOURCES.md`.

   `what` (and the manual's item number), `attaches` (must touch), `visible` (exterior | through | hidden), `source`.
   Names ending in `*` match a prefix (`"Scope*"`). `a.spec("<name>")` makes the build check all of it.
3. `lune run kit trace <image> --name x --spec refs/<name>.spec.json [--exclude box --protect box --part name=box]`

   removed (a bipod) as its own outline. Transparent PNGs trace from alpha (exact). Fails on perspective / wrong

   vetted, pinned, free, offline): `--cutout` takes the object mask from a learned cutout (photos on a REAL background
   trace like studio shots), and `--part-point grip=x,y` traces ONE part's own outline from a single click on it (SAM
   2.1; `--part-box name=x0,y0,x1,y1` from a box; more clicks: `--part-point "grip=x,y;x,y"`, quoted) — a grip, a
   guard, a stock as its exact shape, never "everything in a box". Trace every part you model from its own outline.
   One click on a BIG structure (a stock, a receiver) returns the whole object: the trace then fails "PART = WHOLE
   OBJECT" — give that part a `--part-box`. `--part-text grip="pistol grip"` prompts a part by WORDS (SAM 3, run in
   the TRELLIS.2 environment: the best match; `name="phrase"#2` the 2nd). Every part's mask is JUDGED (trace JSON
   `part_quality`, the summary says "DOUBTFUL part mask(s)", the preview draws them magenta): a box prompt's mask
   filling > 92% of its box (SAM took the region), two masks sharing > 30% of the smaller (grip and guard as one
   area — a real SAM 3 run on a sword gave "sword crossguard" and "sword handle" 99% the same mask), a box-prompted
   mask stopping short while the object runs on as a thin piece (a dropped claw), a plain `--part` box. Retrace a
   doubtful part (more clicks, other words) BEFORE a spec "ref" links it. Spec `gap` features are checked on the
   traced silhouette WITH its openings (a thumbhole, a trigger guard). The trace records where its frame sits in the
   picture: `rbx_trace.px(t, x, y)` turns a pixel you read off the reference into design units exactly. A click that
   lands on the backdrop fails the trace ("PART CLICK OFF THE OBJECT": read clicks off the ORIGINAL picture, not a
   resized copy).

   meet): `rbx_trace.smooth(t, part="grip", flat=0.0003)` samples them as finely as the triangle budget allows (a
   round grip stays round). `--straighten` undoes a photo's perspective; `--ortho` marks a render, a drawing or a flat
   object shot square-on that IS orthographic (a taper is real: no perspective check). A polished blade on a white
   backdrop loses half its width to the backdrop colour — trace it with `--cutout`.
   **Build every part FROM its trace — the hero discipline (the owner, 2026-10-07: "not enough data collected properly
   AND NOT USING THE DATA"):** (a) per part, the SHARPEST picture of the real object you can find: `lune run kit refs
   find "<object> <part>"` fetches originals (up to 40 MB), `refs patent` the drawings, `refs coverage` must say >= 2

   --h a..b --parts p,q` (a mm grid, the outlines). A wrong mask is RETRACED or a better picture found, never modelled

top; FLUX.2 klein, local) to agree the look in the brief — a concept, never a measurement (sizes: real refs).

<real length>` (a library model is rarely at real scale): sizes, loft sections with their squareness (a grip that WRAPS:
`a.loft(stations)`), side / front / top outlines with no camera error. Downloads are vetted by the tool (glTF only,
header + path checks, safe unzip, size caps, CC0 / BY / BY-SA only, licence recorded); library models are references,
never shipped. A staged model's backdrop and loose tools are left out, an animated one is measured assembled; LOOK at

**Then LOOK INSIDE it — not only its outside:** `kit library inspect <uid> --name <name> --length-mm <real length>
[--front min] [--part <words>]` → every part ALONE (`build/library/<name>/parts_sheet.png`: its own size along its own
axes, kind, the openings you can see through it in mm, its material colour / metal / roughness), what touches what,
an assembly animation's take-apart order and each part's way out (relative to the frame), an exploded view, and
`--part safety` = that part from its own side, top, front and 3/4 on a 10 mm grid (a part reference to trace and build
FROM). Read `refs/<name>.inspect.json`: per part it also DISSECTS what the part is made of — `edges` (sharp edges,
bevels measured as the CAD size: chamfer offset / fillet radius, the bevelled share), `surface` (how it was made: cad /
smooth / sculpt_or_scan / faceted), `uv_layout` (islands, seams on hard edges, texture px/mm, stretch, mirroring),
`baked_detail` (what its normal map carries: strength, a repeating pattern's pitch in mm, the relief in mm and its
height picture in `build/library/<name>/detail/` — the sculpt a game model keeps in its textures), `colour_wear`
(colour, roughness / metal ranges, edge wear and grime measured by distance to outward / inward edges). Those numbers
are what real models DO: copy the habits (bevel sizes, seam placement, texture density), not the meshes. A model's
numbers can be stylised (a game model's checkering enlarged): a published dimension outranks them.

kit's knowledge (`kit/learned/<category>.json`: every metric per part role and per finish, as low / typical / high
over the models, each model counting once). `kit learned show firearm` reads it; in a build, `rbx.learned("firearm",
"bevel_size_mm", "role:slide")` gives the typical real value; `kit library inspect build/models/<asset>.blend --name
<asset>_build --length-mm <real length> --out refs/private` + `kit learned compare <asset>_build --category firearm`
lists where YOUR build differs from what 2+ real models agree on (UV seams off hard edges, a too-strong normal map,
too few texture pixels, a pattern at the wrong pitch, edges darker than flats...) — the biggest gap first, with what
to do. A private build's inspection stays in refs/private. The author's part NAMES can be wrong (a 1911 model's "Trigger1" was its
hammer): trust the tile, not the name. And a library model is its author's reading of the object: cross-check a size
that matters against a published dimension before building from it.

-> an outline traced from a reference view (extrude it instead of typing coordinates).

## Stage 2 — 3D draft: generate before you code
**Why:** in a few minutes of GPU per generator (each with the card otherwise idle) a generated draft shows the whole object in 3D before any code
exists — its proportions from every side, what each part looks like where the photos are weak — and for a sculpted
part it IS the shape. Pick by what you have: two to four clean views of the object (a product shot's sides, a chart)
→ `kit gen views` (Pixal3D: the shape lined up with every view — the best start for a real object); one big, clean
3/4 picture → `kit gen <name> --image` (TRELLIS.2); the object as separate pieces → `kit gen parts` (PartCrafter).
Then LOOK at it beside the references and the reference models (`build/gen/` renders; `<name>.gen.json`:
`silhouette_iou` per view; `kit vision similar <render> <photo>`). Use it as: (a) the 3D look-target the code-built
parts must read like together; (b) a proportion check — where the draft, a reference model and the photos disagree,
get a better picture before modelling, never guess; (c) the shape itself for every part whose stack is `generated`.
A generated mesh is one welded surface with soft edges: hard-surface parts with sizes and every MOVING part still come
from code (stage 3), the draft beside them. Measured so far: a whole gun from 4 chart views (Pixal3D) got the overall
shape right with blobby detail; a 145 px side crop gave a ribbed slab — generate from the biggest, cleanest pictures
you have, and a draft that fails (the wrong object, a blob) has cost 5 minutes: say so in the plan and go on.
**Sculpted or ornate? SCULPT it (a.sculpt) or generate it — never build it from boxes.** Heads, creatures, feathers,
engraving, organic or carved shapes and rich surfaces are where code-built models look like blockouts.
**`a.sculpt` — clay in code** (rbx_sculpt: a signed-distance field meshed by Blender's own OpenVDB into ONE closed,
manifold surface — no boolean can fail, no hole can open). In millimetres at real size, Roblox axes, `mirror="x"` for
symmetry: `s = a.sculpt("Head", "skin", voxel_mm=0.5, mirror="x")` → the big masses first (`s.ellipsoid`, `s.ball`,
`s.box(round=)`, `s.capsule(a, b, ra, rb)` tapered limbs and horns) each with `blend=` mm — the fillet a thumb leaves
where two masses meet → secondary forms as strokes (`s.stroke(path, radii, blend=)`: a brow, a tendon, a root — ROUND
in section) or straps (`s.strap(path, height, thickness, round=)`: FLAT in section — a jaw's rising branch, a
cheekbone's arch, a rib, a strap, a rim; bone built from ellipsoid chains read as tubes and slabs) and
carves (`carve=True`: sockets, nostrils, hollows) → creases (`s.crease(path, width, snap=True)`: wrinkles, lids, seams,
sutures — `snap=True` lays a path drawn by eye ON the surface (`s.on_surface(points, depth=)` for any path): a crease
floating over a dome cuts nothing, and the part's report warns of one) → swell a
region (`s.inflate(mm, where=rbx.sculpt_shapes.Ball(...), falloff=)`) → surface relief at its REAL size
(`s.noise(amp_mm, scale_mm, kind=)`: fbm lumps, ridged crests — rock, bark, hide —, cells domes — scales, warts —,
cracks — plates, cracked earth; `where=` a region) → `s.done(adaptivity=0.2, tris=, smooth=)` (smooth: Taubin passes —
the smooth brush, volume kept; `smooth_where=<a shape>`, `smooth_falloff=` mm for one region). An earlier part — a generated
mesh, a loft, a CAD solid — joins as clay with `s.add_part("Name", blend=)` (consumed, its mirrored twins too) and is
remeshed with the rest as ONE surface, carved and detailed like clay. It must be CLOSED to come back whole: an open
mesh comes back closed but hollowed (the build says what share of its volume became clay — fix it with kit make's
clean first). `mirror="x"`: every operation and its reflection (a shape on either side gets its twin). The voxel is the resolution (default: the longest
side / 400): a feature under 1.5 voxels is lost and the build names it (`sculpt_detail`); detail finer than that
belongs in the material (`pattern=`, `scan=`). Meshes are cached by their operations (build/sculpt: an unchanged
sculpt costs nothing; a 200 mm head at 0.5 mm meshes in ~8 s). **Scored as organic:** the share of what is seen that
was sculpted lets surface detail earn `variety` and colour variation earn `materials` (a stump is one bark, its detail
in the surface — not twenty bolted parts in four materials); `Asset(form="organic")` for an imported organic mesh,
`form="hard"` for a machined sculpt. **The form-match TEACHER:** with a checked reference model (kit library get),
`a.form_match(<model.glb>, height=<studs>, rot=, bar_mm=3)` measures the sculpt's surface against the real one's —
never taking it in — and names where the clay must move: EXTRA / MISSING mm per region and HOT SPOTS (model frame,
mm: "missing at [36, 72, 8] mm: 8.2 mm short") and the MIDLINE PROFILE's landmarks ("midline y 102-108 mm: the
build 15 mm in front of the reference: carve, open or move it back") (facts.deep.form_match). PLACE landmarks from
it, never by eye (a skull's bite plane guessed 9 mm high squeezed its maxilla to "a strip pasted under the cheek";
its vault, which the judges called too big, measured within 2-5% of the real one's slices).
**THE SCULPT RECIPE** (proven 2026-10-11: a pure-sculpt human skull, no generators, fresh judges 28 -> 45, the OWNER
75/100 — example `human_skull`, `assets/models/human_skull.override.py`). In this order, each step checked by a NUMBER
on a fast build (`--geo`, minutes), judges only at milestones:
1. MEASURE the reference model first: `R.load`, `R.landmarks` (bite plane, nasion, chin, zygion, gonion...),
   `R.slice_pieces`, its midline profile. Never place a landmark by eye (a guessed bite plane 9 mm high = "a strip
   pasted under the cheek").
2. BLOCK the forms with primitives at measured sizes (balls, ellipsoids, plates, the part kits); the side outline
   from the MESH: `s.trim_to(R.outline(ref, "side"), "side", min_mm=6)` (no picture trace; holes closed, no staircase).
3. FIT the large forms: `s.fit_to(ref, strength=0.6, smooth_mm=15)` — form_match went 1.7 -> 0.85 mm.
4. CHECK the numbers, never a judge's proportion claim alone: form_match extra / missing, midline gaps, hot spots
   ("cranium too big" was false by 2-5%; the real cause was one guessed landmark).
5. SOFTNESS: form_match `crisp` fold share against the reference's — fine relief at the material's scale closes it
   (`s.noise(0.3, 5, kind="ridged")`: 12% -> 21% vs 22.5%); pinched rims alone do not. Then `done(sharp=True)`.
6. COLOUR from the ALBEDO: `kit vision albedo <photo>` -> `kit materials --tones <photo>.albedo.npz` (a photo's own
   pixels carry its shading: 0.49 / 0.39 / 0.24 "olive plastic" vs the albedo's 0.74 / 0.55 / 0.28); its
   grime_tone (stain for bone / ivory / clay).
7. DETAILS LAST: sutures / seams with `crease(snap=True)`; teeth by type with `dental_arch(gap=-0.8)` (they touch);
   brushes for rims.
8. JUDGE when the method changed: `kit judge <asset> --structural "<what changed>"` (a stalled asset gets no packet
   without it). One build: 3-7 min (a 144-operation skull meshes in ~1 min).
**The reference as NUMBERS** (`import rbx_refmesh as R`; plain numpy, the sculpt's frame in mm): `ref = R.load(glb,
height=, rot=)`; `R.landmarks(ref, {"nasion": ("front_min", 110, 130), "zygion": ("side_max", 95, 115), ...})` —
named points by rule; `R.slice_pieces(ref, y)` — EVERY piece of a slice (a holey scan rastered and closed), for
`s.loft_slices([(y, mask, lo, res), ...])` (a form lofted through the real sections); `R.outline(ref, "side")` +
`s.trim_to(outline, "side", min_mm=4)` — the EXACT silhouette, no picture traced. **Sharp, not melted:**
`s.done(sharp=True)` (dual-contouring corners: a box's edges 0.35 -> 0.01 mm), `edge="chamfer"` on any add / carve
(a flat bevel join), `s.brush("pinch" | "flatten" | "inflate" | "crease", path, radius, strength)` (the sculptor's
second pass, headless), `s.strap(...)` (a flat plate — not for bone bars: a strap arch judged worse), the face's
part kits placed by landmarks (`rbx_sculpt_parts.orbit / nasal_aperture / mandible`). **Surface from data:**
`s.stamp("Rock064", tile_mm=, depth=)` (a scan's height map at real size, not noise); `kit materials --tones
<albedo> --mask <cutout>` (base / cavity / edge colours measured, linear) and `grime_tone="stain"` (aged bone, ivory,
clay). **Fit:** `s.fit_to(ref, strength=0.7)` — a smooth correction of the large forms toward the reference after
meshing, the sculpt's own detail kept. **Weld** overlapping parts: `a.sdf_fuse("Body", ["A", ("Bore",
"DIFFERENCE")], voxel_mm=, fillet=)` (Blender's SDF nodes). **Speed:** a 144-operation skull meshes in ~1 min (per-block
pruning, every core) — iterate on `--geo` builds and the numbers (form_match: SOFT, midline gaps, hot spots); judges
only at milestones: a STALLED asset gets no packet without `kit judge <asset> --structural "<what changed>"` (a panel
of 3). Sculpt toward them round by round: the
skull's own sculpt went missing 1.68 -> 0.72 mm (90th percentile 6.0 -> 2.7) in five rounds. Sculpt the WHOLE form against the traced views
(`a.trace_match` judges a sculpted part like any other), then the details, never the reverse. **Start FROM the
references:** `s.from_traces(side="<x>_side", front="<x>_front", blend=)` — the traced views (kit trace) become the
clay's first form, the photos' visual hull (a chest from its two kit make views matched them at IoU 0.994 / 0.996
in a second), then sculpt into it; `mode="keep"` trims a free sculpt back to the photographed silhouette (one view
is enough to trim; `min_mm=4` first drops the outline's islands, spurs and slits thinner than 4 mm — a keep trim runs
through the whole object, and a skull outline's styloid, seen side-on, left fins of clay under its ears; the part's
report warns when an outline has them). `lune run kit gen <name> --image
<picture>` makes the high-detail mesh WITH its own PBR textures on this PC (TRELLIS.2, offline, ~4 min); the build
script imports it into a MASTER: `a.import_mesh("Body", "assets/models/<name>.gen.glb", height=...)` — then cut the
moving pieces out of it, add joints and code-built mechanical parts, `kit optimize` for the game version. Not happy
with the shape: another `--seed`. (Weapons too: the owner's decision on DINOv3's licence, kit/gen/README.md.)
**Sculpted pieces as SEPARATE parts** (a sword's blade, guard, grip, pommel; a lamp's shade, stem, base): `lune run kit
gen parts <name> --image <picture> --parts <n>` (PartCrafter, offline, ~2 min, untextured shapes) writes one GLB per
part + `<name>.parts.json` — each part's box and triangles, and the `a.import_mesh(...)` lines that put the parts back
in place (`L` = the whole object's longest side in studs; give each part its kit material with `mat=`). Each part is
then its own piece to material, joint or cut. Read the boxes first: a part can come out doubled or as a stray shard.
**Licences of generated meshes:** TRELLIS.2 / Pixal3D (`kit gen`, `kit gen views`, `kit gen texture`) are MIT —

**Have the reference chart's views? Generate from ALL of them** — one picture leaves the depth and the proportions to

**R6 OUTFITS — THE OWNER'S OWN FIT IS THE STANDARD** (2026-10-12: their Diablo fit, measured from its OBJ — studs from

trailing to +1.0 at the hem) — it stands out round the body and the legs move INSIDE it. Clean low-poly panels (~40

**CLOTHES (R6 / rigid per body part) — DRAPE, never type a shell** (2026-10-12: the owner called typed box-shells

CHECK IT MOVING, by numbers: `G.coverage(a, {"LeftLeg": "R6LeftLeg", ...})` (each body block's faces covered by ITS

## Stage 3 — Shape: each part by its stack
Each part is made the way the parts plan says (the stack table above) from the data stage 1 gathered — never from
memory. Iterate a CAD part in seconds: `lune run kit cad check <name> --only <Part> [--look]` (no Blender); the
whole model's checks with `lune run kit watch <name> --geo` (a persistent Blender, no bake: seconds on a light asset,
minutes on a CAD-heavy hero — at milestones there); let `kit fit` move the `a.param()` proportions to the traced views
(on a slow build only the few that matter: `--only a,b --evals 24`). A full build only at the stage's end.
**Outlines in code: `g = rbx.profile`** (in a.cad code: `profile`) — `clip_h` / `clip_f` (a traced outline cut to a
part's range), `span_at` / `vspan`, `edge` (a measured edge's MEDIAN: the trace's pixel noise averaged out), `simplify`,
`between`, `rotate` / `shift`, `catmull` (a measured section swept through its keys with no crease — each polyline key
reads as a hard edge), `smooth_run` (a traced strap without its pixel wiggle; join it where the trace is straight: a
kink made a solid whose mesh disagreed with it), `u_floor` / `u_section` (a channel or trough with a half-ellipse
floor). **A part can match every view and still be the wrong shape inside** — the Glock's frame was solid where the
real one is a U-shell: `kit refs sections <name> --ref <library id>` cuts the build beside its reference model, and
`kit plan` asks for it on a hero with a reference model. `kit cad check` FAILS a part whose mesh does not hold its
solid's own volume (`volume_untrue`: it tears open on import while looking closed).
**From the gathered data to the shape** — the real-object list of stage 1 continues here (its steps 4-6; (c)-(e)
carry on its hero discipline (a)-(b)):
**Order of work: silhouette first.** Blockout (the outline, primary forms) → secondary forms → detail. While any
traced view (`a.trace_match`) is below its bar, a hero / aaa build is a BLOCKOUT: it FAILS on `stage` with one next
step — "silhouette first: side IoU 0.81 < 0.9 — fix the outline before detail work" — and the detail-level problems
(part review, texel density, colour, palette, quality score, internal lines, ...) are reported as deferred, not piled
on. The stage is measured, never declared; `Asset(stage_gate=True)` turns it on for a prop, `False` off (`declared`).
4. Model from the trace: `rbx_trace.region(outline, u0, u1, h0, h1, length_mm=L, holes=t.holes)` (clean pieces, never
   the bridges `clip` leaves on concave outlines; without `holes=` the trace's openings are FILLED — a guard opening
   came back solid and buried the trigger), `T.hull(hole)` for an opening traced around something solid, `T.clean`.
   `a.trace_match("<trace>", ignore=(groups the trace excluded), dont_care=[boxes where the reference is unreliable])`
   — the side silhouette must reach IoU >= 0.9 (hero / aaa: until every traced view does, the `stage` gate holds the
   detail checks back — fix the outline first). Trace and match EVERY view you have (`view="front" | "back" | "top"`,
   one call each): each is also measured EDGE TO EDGE — warning `view_gaps_<view>` names the worst 3 places beyond
   3 mm or 2% of the view's size, in mm with `a.real_scale` ("front view: the model is 13 mm too wide at the right (top
   to bottom)", "the reference has 59 mm more at the top front"), per region with `regions=`; numbers in
   `facts.references[].gaps`, places in yellow on `build/renders/<name>_ref_<view>.png`. A top trace runs back -> front
   like a side view (the model is turned to it). Every build then also runs the INTERNAL LINES check (`lines_<view>`,
   below): the lines inside the outline must match the photo's too — with `a.real_scale(mm_per_stud)` it says where a
   missing line is in MILLIMETRES (from the back, from the bottom).
   (c) `lune run kit refs profiles <trace> --anchor <part> --length-mm <real length>` -> every part in mm in the
   build's own frame; build each part from it (a.cad: `clean_profile(r)` makes a traced outline machinable,
   `fillet_soft(edges, r)` takes the largest radius OpenCASCADE allows and says so). Measure big straight edges
   (`median` of the traced edge) instead of copying pixel noise; a feature no picture shows (an ear round a pin) is
   added and said so. A traced outline that will be ROUNDED: build it from polygons (booleans stay robust on flat
   walls), then `smooth_profile()` as the sketch's last step — each run between corners refit as one smooth curve, the
   corners kept (`add(outline(pts))` for a single outline) — not `clean_profile`: its arcs at every traced point, tighter
   than the fillet, refuse it (the Glock frame: every edge refused, even alone). A part made of several widths (a
   frame: grip, receiver, guard) is ONE solid: the smoothed profile extruded once, intersected with a width ENVELOPE
   (the grip's loft + flat blocks) — joined as bodies, their curved walls coincide along the profile and the join lost
   7.7 cm3, or (fuzzy) dropped the whole trigger guard. A piece that must be rounded on its own is its own body with
   its outline INSET ~0.15 mm (no wall coincides), joined plainly; a CAD part that comes apart raises, never keeps the
   largest piece in silence. Extrude a body from its middle (`extrude(amount=w / 2, both=True)`: extruded one way, its
   far side's edges refused radii its near side took); round a body's long edges as ONE set (a set refused whole:
   test each edge alone, round the takers one by one); a corner rounded in plan tighter than the edge's fillet refuses
   it. a.cad's `tol` is millimetres off the true surface; each mesh is checked against its solid's volume. (d) `a.trace_match(<trace>, view="side_left" | "side", parts={"<traced>": ["<Model>", ...]})`:
   each part's own outline (IoU >= 0.85, edges within 1 mm or 4%) — the gate `part_match_<view>` FAILS hero / aaa.
   `pose={"Hammer": 30}` when the picture shows a moving part elsewhere (fit the angle on the picture, never move the
   rest pose to suit it); `"trigger!"` compares outlines only when its holes differ ON PURPOSE; `dont_care=` boxes
   (the design's own features the photo's gun lacks) are left out of the WHOLE-OUTLINE comparison and of the fit — a
   part listed in `parts=` is still compared whole, so leave out of `parts=` a part whose own shape differs on purpose.
   `align="<traced part>"` (a key of `parts=`): lay the model on the picture by THAT part instead of the whole
   outline's box — when one part sticks out differently in the picture (a cocked hammer, a longer beavertail, a game
   design's extra), the box fit shifts and scales every other part; align on a big part both show whole (a can, a
   barrel, a receiver). `a.project(<same trace>)` then paints with the same alignment. (e) Two
   references disagree (a real gun and the game's version of it)? The design's own picture rules its own features
   (sights, hammer, grip), the real object's photo everything else — written in the build's docstring, each
   `dont_care` / left-out part with its reason. Lay a design picture over the real photo first (best-fit offset and
   length: the Silverballer render lands on the Hardballer at IoU 0.92) — a guessed alignment shifts every part.
   **Part sizes in mm, every build:** `lune run kit refs views <name>` measures every traced part; a spec part with
   `"ref": "<traced part>"` is then set beside it (gate `ref_sizes`: what is SEEN of the part from the side — rays from
   both flanks — against the traced mask, frames lined up on all linked parts; off = more than 5% or 3 mm). Link only
   parts whose masks are CLEAN: a box prompt's SAM mask can fill the box or run into its neighbour (the Gjallarhorn's
   grip and guard were one area, its crown lost a claw) and `--part name=box` is the whole object inside the box.
   `kit refs views` carries kit trace's judgement into the views file; a ref to a doubtful mask is named by the
   `ref_sizes_doubtful` warning ("ref 'grip' is a doubtful mask: fills its box ... — retrace it").
5. A 3D reference at real scale (a CC0 scan, any glTF / FBX / OBJ): `lune run kit refs measure <model> --name x` ->
   `refs/x.measure.json` (size in mm, cross-sections) + side / front / top outlines with NO camera error
   (`assets/models/x_<view>.trace.json`). Build from them: `a.carve(side=..., top=..., front=...)` (three views: IoU
   >= 0.99 per view on a measured shape) or `a.loft("Body", rbx_trace.loft_stations(rbx_trace.measure("x"), L), mat)`
   (sections within 0.2%).
6. PROPORTIONS the references should decide are `a.param()` numbers — `L = a.param("barrel_length", 2.4, lo=1.8,
   hi=3.0)` — and `lune run kit fit <name>` moves them to match the asset's REFERENCES (silhouette IoU per view and
   proportions, CMA-ES over --geo builds in a persistent Blender) — never the quality score (a fit that chased it would
   buy clutter, not resemblance). `--write` keeps the values beside the script (`<name>.params.json`, used by every
   later build; `facts.params` lists them). A synthetic case comes back within 2% of its true values.

**Code CAD for hard-surface parts** (`a.cad`, build123d on OpenCASCADE — `lune run kit tools setup cad` once): a
receiver, a barrel, a rail, a ring, a grip — anything machined — as an EXACT solid: fillets, chamfers, holes, shells,
sweeps, lofts, threads come out clean at the size the code says (no boolean slivers, no guessed bevels). The code is
millimetres in the kit's axes (x right, y up, z back: the front is -Z), build123d's names already imported, the part
left in `part`; measured numbers go in as `params` (`P` in the code). 1 stud = `a.real_scale()` mm (else 280).
**Real fasteners and springs (down to the screws):** every a.cad part script has kit/tools/cad_parts.py's helpers —
`screw("M3-0.5", 10, head="socket"|"button"|"countersunk"|"pan"|"hex", threads=False)` (ISO tables; its head's
top at z 0, its shank down -Z), `nut("M3-0.5")`, `pin(d, length)`, `roll_pin(d, length)`, `spring(mean_d, wire_d,
length, coils)` (a wire swept along a helix, ground ends), `clearance("M3")` (the ISO 273 hole: cut it in the host:
`part = Box(...) - Rot(90, 0, 0) * Cylinder(clearance("M4") / 2, 40)`). Sizes from `kit refs dims find "iso 4762
m3"`. Gate `fastener_seat` (an error at hero / aaa): a screw / pin / nut / spring / a.internal() part with more than a
third of its volume inside another part's SOLID has no hole or cavity — cut it; `fastener_support` catches the
opposite (a pin spanning air). Beyond these: `from bd_warehouse.fastener import ...` (washers, set screws,
`bd_warehouse.thread`, bearings, gears).
**Shapes that WRAP around (gate `section_match`):** a grip, a stock, a shroud is not an extruded box. `kit refs measure
<model>` records the reference's horizontal slices (`levels`: at each fraction of the height the biggest island's
width, depth and squareness — 2 = an ellipse, 6+ = a box; a real 1911 grip reads ~2). Build FROM them in an a.cad
part: `part = loft_sections(from_levels(P["levels"], height_mm, length_mm, hs=(0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4)))` with
`params={"levels": <the measure.json levels>}` (also `section(w, d, squareness)`, `loft_sections([...], axis="z")`
along the length). Check it: `a.section_match("<ref>", levels=(0.15, 0.3, 0.45))` slices the build the same way and
names a slice boxier than the reference's (by > 1 squareness) or of other proportions (> 25%). Measure the reference
facing right (`--front min` when its muzzle points left), or the grip lands at the front.
**Curves, not polygons (gate `faceting`, an error at hero / aaa):** a curve is judged by how far its facets sit from
the true curve (the sagitta) — hero 0.08 mm, prop 0.25 mm: 16 sides are fine on a 3 mm pin (0.03 mm), not on a 40 mm
barrel (0.38 mm). Give a.cylinder / a.tube enough `segments`, or build it in a.cad (tessellated to 0.05 mm). Real
models: typically 0.067 mm (`kit learned show firearm`, facet_sag_mm). UVs: the kit unwraps every group itself
(smart projection, 66 deg): its seams fall on corners and nothing stretches past ~1.2x — check a build's per part
with `kit library inspect build/models/<asset>.blend ...` (uv_layout: seams_across_flats, stretched_share, px/mm)
and `kit learned compare`.
**Bevel everything (gate `sharp_edges`, an error at hero / aaa):** every visible part's hard edges (> 30 deg) are
measured like a reference model's (kit/refs/dissect.py): the share that is bevelled must reach half at hero (a quarter
on a prop) — and with `a.learned_from("firearm")` what REAL models of that kind do for that part's role (a frame:
~96%), the fix naming their typical bevel size (`kit learned show firearm`: bevel_size_mm by role). Round edges in
CAD (`round_edges`, `fillet_soft(edges, r)`; `fillet_each(hard_edges(), r)` rounds exactly what is STILL sharp —
two faces meeting at > 30 deg, one edge at a time — and leaves a fillet's own tangent seams alone: a screw's slot
lips, a turned part's corners, a magazine's outside; a fused feature a fillet breaks (OpenCASCADE refuses it, the solid
goes invalid) gets its corners chamfered in its own sketch instead), in boxes / extrusions (`bevel=r`). When OpenCASCADE REFUSES a fillet
outright, a.cad bevels the part's still-sharp edges on the mesh instead (at most 12% of its thinnest side, 4
segments) and says so (`cad[].bevel_fallback_mm`) — never a sharp edge left in silence.
**The fast loop: `lune run kit cad check <asset>`** runs every a.cad part of the build script in SECONDS, without
Blender (closed solid? volume, faces, open edges, fillets it had to shrink, or the error / crash line). A build is
minutes: never debug a part's CAD inside builds — check, fix, check, and build only when every part is ok.
`--look` also DRAWS each part (four sides, Blender, seconds: build/cad/<asset>_<Part>.png; `--zoom cx,cy,cz,size`
in the part's mm) — see a fin, a step or a fillet that did not take before any build. Openings in a plate (a trigger
guard's): union their shapes in the 2D PROFILE before extruding and round the edges in the same pass — 3D cuts of
different widths, and box cuts thinning a rounded part, leave knife-edge fins at curved corners.

## Stage 4 — Surface: every part's finish from data
Every part gets the surface its references show — not a default grey: its finish (`rbx.finish`, `rbx.real`, a scan),
its pattern at the real pitch, its markings engraved at their measured place, its wear where use puts it — and the
colours measured off the most neutrally lit picture. Generators and projection paint what no material imitates.
**Start from a LOOK** (`lune run kit materials --looks [query]`, kit/materials/looks.json; SEE them all built and
baked with a life: `lune run kit materials --looks --sheet` -> build/materials/looks/sheet.png): a real surface's whole
recipe — its base values (a kit finish, a measured material, a scan's measured mean or a labelled estimate: every look
says which), the scan that breaks it up, its micro-pattern at real size, how much its colour varies, which grime it
shows: `a.material("stock", **rbx.look("walnut_wood"), life="used")`, `**rbx.look("powder_coat", color=(0.3, 0.05,
0.04))` — ANY key overridden inside `rbx.look(...)` (passing a look's key to a.material again is Python's "multiple
values" error). 46 looks: the Glock's nitride slide and polymer frame, parkerized, blued, anodized (black, or any
colour on aluminium), brushed stainless, scratched steel, brushed / matte (bead-blasted) / turned aluminium, cast iron, worn brass,
copper, chrome, car paint, powder coat, enamel, ABS, rubber, rubber flooring, rust, rusted steel, oak, walnut,
cherry, mahogany, dark and furniture wood, end grain, bark (fir), oak and mossy bark, leather, bone, skin, concrete,
rock, brick, marble, porcelain, canvas, burlap, cloth, wool, nylon webbing. **No flat colour:** `vary=`
(0..1) moves a material's colour about its own value — a board's tone drifting along it, an oxide's mottling — +-16%
over ~120 mm, +-8% over ~25 mm and a warm / cool drift, object-space (seamless), its average kept (every look has
one). **End grain** (`pattern="endgrain"`, `pattern_mm=` a year, `grain=` the tree's axis): a sawn trunk's or log's
concentric rings and darker heart (`a.zone` the cut face). **Dust:** `grime_tone="dust"` — a light grey-beige in the
recesses, the only grime a dark finish can show (the default dirt is a dark brown that darkens). **Wear where USE
is:** `a.contact("hand" | "rub" | "ground", at=, size=)` — boxes (studs) the bake's wear follows: a hand smudges and
polishes what it holds and wipes its grime off, a holster rubs flats and burnishes edges, the ground gathers dirt and
scuffs where it rests (facts.contacts: each kind's share of the map).
**Markings are engraved, never painted from a lit picture:** each marking at its measured place and size on the
picture (`rbx_trace.px` turns its pixels into design units): `a.cut(<part>, a.text(..., "engraving", depth=...,
facing=...))` — the same metal both sides (projecting a lit render onto metal gave the Silverballer two different
textures). A logo: trace its close-up (`kit trace`) and cut its outline in; a printed or painted mark: `a.decal`.
Code-built surfaces get real detail from SCANS: `a.material(..., scan="Metal008", scan_mix=0.6)` (rbx_scans: the
owner-approved CC0 scans — metal, rust, wood species, bark, cloth, leather, plastic, concrete, rock, bricks; `color` /
`rough` stay the averages; a metal scan's own metal map says where it is NOT metal — rust streaks on steel).
`lune run kit materials --scans` lists them with their real tile size; more is a DOWNLOAD: the owner approves the
exact ids, then `kit materials --fetch <ids>` (size checked against ambientCG's API, sha256 pinned — a re-fetch must
match it —, only ambientCG's hosts over https, maps only) and a VETLOG.md row.
**Pattern vs texels:** a real-pitch pattern (`pattern_mm=`: grip texture, end-grain rings) needs 4+ px of map a cell
— gate `pattern_resolution` names one the bake would blur to mottling (more texels: `tex=`, the part in its own
`group=`; or a coarser pitch where the real object allows). A sculpt's (and a dense imported mesh's) UVs follow its
FORM, not its relief (rbx_uvcharts: six charts by the smoothed normal, conformal): a smart projection cut the stump's
bark into islands that left 0.11% of its map on the surface (0.05 px/mm; now 1.19).
   **Colours:** `lune run kit vision albedo <the traced picture> -o <file>.npz` (Marigold-IID: its colour with the
   lighting taken out, roughness, metalness), then `a.trace_match(..., albedo=<file>.npz,
   regions="refs/<name>.regions.json")` — gate `colour_match` compares the model's colour map with it region by region
   (CIELAB delta E; it says how: "darker by 16 L*", "green where the reference is blue"; build/renders/<name>_colours_<view>.png)
   from EVERY traced view that has its albedo + regions — side, side_left, front, back, top (a.trace_match(view=...)).
**Colours from pictures, ungraded:** before sampling a colour from a reference, `lune run kit refs colour <picture> [--neutral x0,y0,x1,y1] [--sample grip=x0,y0,x1,y1]` — a render or a photo is often colour-GRADED (the owner's Silverballer side render: a blue cast of chroma 8.4 — black rubber sampled as navy). It measures the cast on a surface you know is neutral (else the least colourful mid-tones), balances the picture and gives each sample raw and balanced: build with the balanced one. Choose a measured material by looking: `kit materials --sheet` (all 86 on spheres as rbx.real() gives them).
Real materials: 86 measured substances (physicallybased.info, CC0; `lune run kit materials [query]`); the critic's
`material_values` warns when a material NAMED like one ("gold", "brass", "copper", "silver") looks nothing like it —
use `rbx.real(...)` or name the surface it really has (`painted_gold`, `dark_brass`); steel and iron are not judged
(their finishes range from mirror to blackened). **Physically based values** (`pbr_values`, rbx_pbr — an ERROR at
hero/aaa): metalness is 0 or 1; a metal's base colour is 180-255 sRGB (its reflectance), a non-metal's 30-240 (aaa:
50+). A dark "gunmetal" is never a dark metal — real ones are COATINGS (parkerizing, bluing, paint, anodizing: metal
0); a darker bare metal (150-180) only when rough. Pick from `rbx.finish(...)`: `gunmetal_parkerized` · `blued_steel` ·
`black_anodized` · `nitride` (a pistol slide: near-black, slick) · `painted_metal` (color=) · `enamel` · `pearl_paint` ·
`polymer` · `rubber` · `machined_steel` · `dark_steel` · `brushed_aluminium` · `chrome` · `polished_brass` ·
`cast_bronze`. `satin=True`: a partial metalness on purpose; `pbr=False`: stylised on purpose (listed in facts.pbr).
**What a life does to it — `life=`, on ANY material:** `a.material("slide", **rbx.finish("nitride", life="used"))`,
`a.material("wall", **rbx.real("Brick"), life="used")`, `a.material("x", color=..., rough=..., life="heavy")` — the
wear keys for the surface's class, worked out from its own values (a bare metal; a dark coating: edges burnished to a
satin grey 2.5 mm wide, flats rubbed in patches; a dark polymer / rubber: the same, softer; paint (`under=` given):
chips; a lighter non-metal — wood, stone, fabric: dirt in its recesses and handling marks). `"used"` is the wear the
owner rated on the Glock (85/100); `"new"` · `"light"` half of it · `"heavy"` half again more. Every wear key you also
give a.material wins: `life="used", rub=0.8` (a holster zone). **Dark finishes** (`dark_finishes`, a warning): two big dark non-metals under 15 sRGB apart read as ONE mass
(the Glock's frame at 57 under its 47 slide: "the same blue-navy" to two fresh judges; the rated build: frame 86 —
`color=(0.095, 0.094, 0.092), rough=0.72` — under the slide's 47); on a hero, a big dark finish with no burnish / rub
is named too (eight judges read a bare one as "no wear at all"). **Roughness variation** (`roughness_variation`,
measured on the BAKED roughness map, material by material): a material on 5%+ of the surface that is flat at two of
three scales (fine / mid / coarse) is named — flat roughness reads as plastic; give it `scan=`, `pattern=` (fine) or
`wear=` + `grime=` (mid, coarse). Style: pictures of OTHER objects in the wanted style
(a CC0 pack, the kit's starters, concept art — never the object itself: that is a reference); every full build
reports how close the look (SigLIP 2) and the outline (solidity, fill, aspect vs the style's range) come
(`build/renders/<name>_style.png`). A report, never a gate.
**Surface** is automatic and scale-aware: edge highlight radius and default bevels follow the model's size; wear,
chips and polish land on CONVEX exposed edges (curvature from the baked normal), dirt in concave corners and
crevices. Raise `chip=`/`wear=`/`grime=` per material for a used look; `pattern=` adds micro-relief (hex · weave ·
brushed · grid · coat · engraved). **The sculpt at REAL size:** `pattern="checkering"` (a grip's diamonds), `"knurl"`
(a round part's rolled diamonds), `"stipple"` (punched dimples), `"pebble"` (a rubber grip's bumps) with
`pattern_mm=<real pitch>` — the cell comes from the group's MEASURED texture density, so 20 lines per inch is 1.27 mm
on the model (measured back off the export by `kit library inspect`: test_patterns.py). Take the pitch from a source
(`kit refs dims`), not from a game model (one 1911 model's checkering was 6.2 mm — stylised). Under ~4 px a cell is
too fine for the map (the bake's facts say so: `resolved`): give the part more texture or its own group.
**Engraved:** `pattern="engraved"` — ornamental scrollwork (S-scrolls, a vine,
leaves) with dark tarnish in its grooves, for engraved bronze / chased silver; give it its own material (the plain
trims stay plain). Its grooves shade like relief, so the score counts them as crease detail. **Wood:** `pattern="wood"` — grain along each part's own long axis and a tone per part, so
make EVERY board its own part (small gaps over a dark core make the seams): see the crate and treasure_chest starters.
**Shape right, surface wrong? Paint it from the reference.** When a code-built model's form matches but its surface
reads as flat colour against the photo (a leather wrap, worn two-tone paint, a dark grip on bright steel), TRELLIS.2
paints the asset's OWN last full build from the picture: `lune run kit gen texture <asset> --image <picture>
[--turn <deg>] [--group <g>]` -> `assets/models/<asset>.tex.glb`; then `a.texture_from("<asset>.tex.glb", mix=1.0,
channels=("color", "rough", "metal"))` before `a.build()` bakes it onto the atlas (closest surface; the kit's AO, wear,
grime and edge normals still go on top). `--turn`: stand the object in the picture as the model stands (a trace's
`rotate`). Read `facts.texture_from` (coverage %, mean difference per map) and LOOK at the sheet. Limits: 512 —
colour regions, not lettering or engraving (relief / displace); the unseen sides are guessed; it decides what is metal
(`channels=("color",)` keeps the kit's roughness/metal); albedo, so a lit photo's leather comes out darker; a shape
edit after painting drops the coverage (`texture_coverage` warns) — paint again. Details: kit/gen/README.md.

**Paint it with its references** (rbx_project): when the look IS the reference — a stylised hero piece, an
engraved or painted relic, markings and wear no material imitates — project the traced pictures onto the model and
bake them into its colour map: `a.project("<name>_side")` (+ `a.project("<name>_top", view="top")`, one call per
registered picture). Each picture paints only what its camera sees FIRST (a depth test: a part behind another gets
nothing), fades on faces turned away (`facing=`), never takes the backdrop (the outline's edge, `feather=` px, takes
the colour from just inside), and pictures blend by how squarely a face looks at each. A side picture with the front
on the right paints the right side (+X); front on the left, the left side; `mirror=True` paints the other side
mirrored (lettering reads backwards). Framing is `trace_match`'s: the model's silhouette box (without the trace's
`ignore=` groups) is the outline's box — so fit the silhouette first (IoU >= 0.9), then paint.
**Colour or detail?** The COLOUR mode (default) paints the picture's own colours: right for a flat-lit or albedo
picture whose colours ARE the object's (a painted logo, a livery, a stylised piece drawn flat). A LIT picture (a studio
render, a photo, chart views each in their own light) is the wrong source of colour — its shading and shadows become
albedo: on the owner's hero piece a studio side render + three chart views (mix 0.85, flatten 0.4) left the model
darker and muddier than its own physically based materials. For those use DETAIL mode:
`a.project("<name>_side", detail=1.0, bump=1.0)` — every texel keeps its material's colour and takes only the
picture's fine structure (engraving, seams, filigree, small markings, light-independent: thin features of its LOG
luminance, a morphological top-hat — gradients, big shadows and shadow edges give ~0; normalised per picture, re-centred
so each material's mean stays within a few /255); `bump=` puts the same lines into the normal map (dark = deep);
`detail_px=` the widest feature still counted as detail (default 5 texels). The verdict's `material_shift_255` says
per picture how far it moved each material (measured: the same lit picture moved bronze -37/255 in colour mode, 0 in
detail mode). Detail limits: a dark PAINTED line and an engraved groove read the same (both become darker + deeper);
a shadow's soft edge can leave a faint trace where a line crosses it. One marking only, not the whole picture:
`a.decal("serial", "<name>_side", box=(x0, y0, x1, y1))` (picture pixels; 4 corners straighten a marking shot at an
angle; `at=(u, h)`, `size=(w, h)` in design units, `picture=` a close-up). Limits: the picture's LIGHT is baked in
(`flatten=0.6` takes broad gradients and big shadows out — crude: small shading stays, a dark paint reads as
shadow); only faces some picture sees get its colour (the rest keep the material — add a view or a decal); `mix<1`
keeps the material's scans, patterns and wear under it; roughness and metal stay the material's; the trace needs a
frame (traced without `--rotate` / `--straighten`: turn the picture FILE itself if it lies sideways). The verdict says
per picture how much it covered, whether the picture is still where its trace says (`projection_alignment`: a picture
cropped or edited after tracing) and how the finished model looks from that camera against the picture
(`projection_match`; LOOK at build/renders/<name>_project_<trace>.png: the picture above, the model below).
**Ornaments FROM the reference** (a wing, a crest, engraving: code cannot sculpt them, a generator smears them):
cut the ornament out (`lune run kit vision parts <picture> --text "wing"`, SAM 3; or `vision mask --point x,y`), then
`lune run kit vision relief <cut-out.png> -o assets/models/<name>.relief.npz` (MoGe-2 normals + Depth Anything 3,
integrated; LOOK at build/vision/<name>.relief_preview.png) and build it at its measured place:
`a.relief("Wing", npz, "bronze", width=..., depth=..., pos=..., rot=(0, -90, 0), bend=...)` (both_sides=True: a
bilateral bust from a side profile — it reads as a lump on a head: use a form + displace instead). A part that already
has its big form (a lofted head) takes the picture's fine sculpting with `a.displace("Head", npz, amount, width=...,
pos=..., rot=...)` — call it BEFORE the part's cuts, and no tiny (1-2 mm) cuts on the dense result.

## Stage 5 — Mechanism: every moving part, and the take-apart
Every part that moves in the real object is its own part with its joint (`a.joint` in the build calls of stage 3:
slide, hinge, fixed; `after=` for a sequence), enclosed parts in `with a.internal():`, game attachment points with
`a.anchor`, bores and sight lines kept clear with `a.clear_path`. Declare the take-apart early: it finds geometry
errors no other check sees.
**Take-apart (assembly / disassembly)** — an object made of real parts comes apart in a real order, down to its
screws: declare it once every part exists, like a manual's field strip + detail strip:
```python
a.disassembly([
    {"name": "magazine out", "parts": ["Magazine", "Follower", "Round*"], "move": (0, -0.6, 0.2)},   # studs
    {"name": "rounds out of the magazine", "parts": ["Round*"], "path": [(0, 0.3, 0), (0, 0, -0.2)]},  # moves again
    {"name": "grips off", "parts": ["GripPanel"], "move": (0.1, 0, 0), "outward": True},  # left goes left
    {"name": "barrel out", "parts": ["Barrel"], "path": [                  # a part held at BOTH ends TURNS out:
        {"turn": 12, "axis": (1, 0, 0), "pivot": at(183.9, BORE_H)},       # tipped about the ring holding its muzzle,
        mv(df=-10, dh=-2.1), mv(dh=-40)]},                                  # back along its own axis, then down
], base=["Frame", "Magazine"])                       # what everything comes off (sub-assemblies' bases too)
```
The build checks every step comes out CLEAR along its path against where the other parts are at that step ("Barrel
runs into Frame at 4% of its path" = a real collision: take that part first, change the path, or cut the clearance
the real part has — a dovetail, an open slot), and that every part comes off (`disassembly`, an error at hero / aaa).
A jam says WHERE ("around (x, y, z) studs": the middle of the mover's colliding faces) — read it as the part's own
units and you see what is missing (the Silverballer's: a 1911 nose's keyhole for the barrel's lug, the rear sight's
dovetail, a grip safety's pocket open to the rear, a follower 0.2 mm wider than its magazine). A part taken off must
leave the others' way clear: out, then ASIDE (`"path": [mv(df=60), mv(dh=60)]` — a bushing parked in front of the
slide stops the barrel). SOFT parts squeeze past instead of jamming: a spring (named ...Spring) compresses, rubber /
silicone / leather / fabric / foam (the kit material its faces use) stretches — the step's row says `squeezed`, the
gate's line names it ("GripRubber squeezed past Frame"); the same part in steel jams.
It renders the steps (`build/renders/<asset>_strip.png`) and exports the ASSEMBLY: every part its own mesh with its
maps (`build/models/<asset>_assembly.fbx`; the steps in the manifest). Pins and screws need real holes to come out
through: a pin buried in a wall cannot be pushed out.
`lune run kit asset <asset> --schematic` renders the mechanism's views (asked for since 2026-11-11: every full build
rendered them, 17 of one hero's 20 minutes; the clearance check itself runs on every build), then
`lune run kit schematic <asset>` -> three sheets: GA drawing (third angle, hatched section, dimensions, parts list),
mechanism (exploded + every joint at full travel), model sheet. A kept set of views is marked stale when the model
changes; `kit schematic` then asks for them again instead of drawing an old model.

## Stage 6 — Look and judge: once, then one fix round
The full build (`lune run kit asset <name>`), LOOKED at beside the reference pictures — the sheet, the region
close-ups, `kit vision differ <render> <photo>` — then fresh judges and ONE fix list. The score below is a helper that
counts parts, triangles, materials and creases; it is never the bar of a hero build: the judges' is.
Score components (each in the build report, `facts.quality`):
| component | what it measures | how to raise it |
|---|---|---|
| detail | crease density on flat-lit renders | the secondary forms the REAL object has: plates, inset panels, panel lines, vents on hard surface; boards, battens, hardware on wood |
| empty | blank expanses: no creases AND no visible texture (listed biggest-first WITH coordinates) | give exactly those places what the real object has there: texture, hardware, markings |
| variety | distinct KINDS of small detail (copies don't count) | screws, latches, LEDs, connectors, knurling, cables, text |
| geometry | triangles vs the tier budget (hero ~9k, aaa ~16k; >1.5x is penalised; Roblox caps 20k per mesh) | spend on forms; the report lists the biggest triangle costs |
| surface | edge normals + AO + edge wear + chipping + grime | `material(..., chip=0.4, under=(metal), grime=0.4, wear=0.2)` |
| texture | the texture a player SEES: band-pass contrast of the albedo views (grain, wear, grime, chips, stencils), full credit at 1.2% median — more earns nothing | `pattern="wood"`, `wear=`, `chip=`, `grime=` in crevices, stencils |
| materials | 3-5 zones with clear value steps | frame / panels / rubber / metal / accent |
| markings | text, serials, stripes (hero/aaa) | `a.text(...)`, `P.stripes(...)` |
| silhouette / reference | outline breaks; match to `reference={"side": ...}` | proportions first |
| **craft** | plausibility — a FACTOR on the whole score (named first whenever it costs): wood carved instead of built (4+ grooves / recesses, 8+ carved regions of any shape, or 8%+ of the wood's surface
carved — each cut's own faces are traced through the boolean in every material of the cutter, so a comb carving 37
grooves is 37, fused of two materials or not; wood is judged per face (a block fused into a steel nub is still wood);
lines baked on wood — floater seams — count with the grooves; the wood's surface is what is SEEN, internal parts
left out, plus the walls carved into it, so carving can never carve a model out of being wooden), see-through slits in a mostly-wooden model | separate boards over a dark core; `rbx.Asset(see_through=True)` when gaps ARE the design (a slatted crate, a grille); `a.cut(..., through=True)` when a split is (a clamp ring) |
The score measures DETAIL, not resemblance — proportions and shape language come from the comparison image and
your critique. Use `reference={"side": "refs/x_side.png"}` too when you have a side view (silhouette match %).
**The weights are the OWNER's:** fitted to their blind grades (`kit/grades.json`) and every raw score is mapped onto
their scale. **You never grade — the owner does:** `lune run kit grade <asset> <0-100> "why"` asks them to type the
score again at their own terminal (an agent's shell has none: it refuses), and a blind round they graded on a page
comes in with `lune run kit grades --import <round.json>` — also confirmed by the owner at their terminal, every grade
naming its page and the build it showed (script sha, triangles), a round only once (kit/grading/README.md).
`lune run kit grades` shows how well the score follows them, leave-one-out (each grade predicted without itself), and
the current weights on FRESH grades given after they were fitted (the prospective test); `--write` keeps a refit only
when no metric's own test (a sign test on same-object pairs, McNemar on pass/fail, a bootstrap on rank) says it is
worse than the hand weights (p < .05) and one says it is better (p < .0914 each, so the three together keep a
no-better refit at most one time in four, however many grades there are), so fresh blind grades are the real test. Grades carry the measuring code that saw them
(`rbx_score.MEASURE_VERSION`), the script that MADE the graded build (its sha, written into the build's history when
it was built) and the model's fingerprint (triangles, size); after the measuring code changes, `lune run kit grades
--remeasure` rebuilds every graded build it can find and refreshes it — unless its script changed or it no longer
builds the model the owner saw (the owner's numbers never change).
**The ratchet:** `lune run kit accept <asset> "the owner's words"` locks the build the OWNER approved as a floor — a
later build measuring more than 2 raw points below it FAILS (re-scored with the weights of the day, so a refit never
fails an unchanged build). A floor measured by other measuring code cannot be compared, so it vouches only for its OWN
build: one MADE by its script (the file Blender ran, sha recorded at build time) that is its model (the fingerprint it
kept) passes, warned to re-base; any other build FAILS until the floor is re-based — a floor without a fingerprint
vouches for nothing. Lowering a floor (a drop of more than the 0.1 rebuild noise: small steps cannot walk it down) or
moving it onto another build is the OWNER's: `--lower`, confirmed at their terminal; the one re-base without them is
that same build under new measuring code. Once a floor is COMMITTED, the committed one holds (found whatever the
spelling or the models folder): a change of floor takes effect when it is committed, and no edit, deletion or breakage
of the file moves it; a floor never committed is only its file — commit floors. An empty or unreadable record, or a
repository git cannot read, FAILS the build; a build measured by older code is never accepted; the record keeps the
floor it replaced. Opt-outs
(`see_through=True`, `cut(..., through=True)`) are listed in every verdict (`declared`). **Honest studio:** the hero render shows true
colour (Khronos PBR Neutral at -0.8 EV, a sky the reflections see) — the selftest renders a chart of known
albedos every run, so what you look at is what the owner gets. **Product studio** (the judge's views, their close-ups and
`kit showcase`; the owner's call, 2026-10-07): reflections see a mid-grey room with bright softbox strips and dark
bands, the way polished steel is photographed — under the honest sky a polished slide read as matte primer. Every
MEASURED render (the sheet, the reference checks, the quality floor) stays honest. Worn edges of a BRIGHT metal
(stainless, chrome) burnish lighter than its flats (+0.25 at the most-rubbed edge); a dark one still wears toward bare
steel.

### Region map (once per reference — this is what makes you SEE)
Whole-model sheets hide small things: a detail that is 3% of the image is invisible to you AND to the judges
(vision models lose small subjects; cropping restores them — ICLR 2025 "MLLMs know where to look"). So split the
reference into regions: `refs/<name>.regions.json`:
```json
{"image": "refs/x.png", "regions": [
  {"name": "scope", "box": [x0, y0, x1, y1], "parts": ["Scope"], "questions": ["Is the scope a short, fat ROUND tube ...?", "..."]}]}
```
`box` = pixels on the reference; `parts` = name prefixes of YOUR parts in that region; 4-6 yes/no `questions` about
concrete visible features (shape, counts, sub-parts, finish, markings) — "yes" = the model matches. Every build then
renders each region close-up from the reference viewpoint (`build/renders/<name>_r_<region>.png`) and `kit judge`
pairs them with the reference crops in `build/judge/<name>/regions.png`. Your review must have a line per region
(`- scope: ...`), and the judges answer every question: the kit keeps a reference checklist % and tells you what was
FIXED and what REGRESSED since the last verdict.

### The look-and-judge round
ONE round (rule 9): the steps below once — the judges' fix list done in it — and another round only for what the
look still shows wrong; never a loop of rebuilds to raise a number. Judges move about ±1.5 on their own: `kit judge
--record` says when a verdict is inside that noise of the best before it, and calls three in a row a PLATEAU (the
Glock's material rounds: 59.5 / 58.5 / 58.2 / 60.1, the panels asking for opposite changes) — change structure, or
show the owner. Judges also score harder than the owner (64 where the owner said 85): a packet shows the owner's own
grade of the asset first (`kit grade`; a private asset's anchor stays on its machine).
1. Shape work: `lune run kit asset <name> --geo` (~15 s on a 95-part rifle) — every geometry, mechanism, reference
   and deep per-part check, no bake / export (own verdict `.verify/asset-geo-<name>.json`; the full one is untouched).
   Then the full build `lune run kit asset <name>` (bake cache): an unchanged model reuses every group's last maps
   + mesh in seconds; a material change re-bakes only the groups that use it; ANY geometry change (a part moved
   more than ~2x the merge tolerance, ~0.3 mm on a rifle; a face changing material; a part changing group)
   re-bakes every group — each group's AO sees every part. Part tiles re-render when anything they show changed
   (shapes, maps, joints, real scale). Since 2026-11-11 both rounds KEEP PICTURES to look at (the summary's `LOOK:`
   line): `--geo` six views in the parts' own colours + a pair per hero feature in `build/judge/<name>/geo/`; the
   full build the same views lit like a product shot (1024 x 768; hero / aaa 1280 x 960) + the pairs in
   `build/judge/<name>/builds/<build id>/`. The schematic's take-apart / line-art views are rendered only when
   asked: `lune run kit asset <name> --schematic`. `--rebake` re-bakes and re-renders everything.
   The report says what was re-baked and why (`bakes.why`) and where the seconds went (`timing`). `--fast` skips sheets.
   `lune run kit watch <name>` rebuilds on every save through a persistent Blender (no start-up per build: ~30% faster,
   identical verdicts) — the fast inner loop; `kit asset` stays the full chain.
2. Read `summary` (score, delta vs the last build) and `next_action` (the weakest component + where) — then OPEN
   the sheet. **The score is a helper, never the judge:** it rewards creases, and a model carved full of grooves to
   lift it read as noise (crate, chest and blaster went from 33-47 to 55-58 and were graded 1/100; the clean versions
   scored lower). Never add detail the real object would not have; never accept a score — yours or an agent's —
   without looking at the hero render.
3. `lune run kit judge <name>`, then LOOK at `build/judge/<name>/regions.png` region by region (reference left,
   model right) and the whole-model `sheet.png`. Never design a part from a description — put it beside its
   reference crop first.
4. Write `assets/models/<name>.review.md`: `iteration: <the build you just saw>`, one `- <region>: ...` line per
   region, and 5+ bullets of what is still worse than the reference and what you change next.
5. Make the changes. Rebuild. Hero/aaa: fresh judges (a panel of 2-3, never the builder) score the packet; record
   with `kit judge <name> --record`. If the panel says WORSE than the previous build, revert that change first.
6. **Is this build better than that one?** Ask it as a PAIR — judges pick between two far more reliably than they
   score one. `lune run kit judge <name> --pair [[A] B]` (A/B: `current`, `accepted`, `judged`, `previous`, `i<N>`, a
   build id; one name = current against it; none = current against accepted, else previous): every full build keeps
   six views (front, back, left, right, top, 3/4 — one sticky camera, the same light), its region close-ups and the
   brief's game camera (`view:` — first person, third person, top-down, in the world) in
   `build/judge/<name>/builds/<build>/`. The packet (`pair/judge_<n>/`, 3 by default) shows each item — `overall`,
   the game camera, every region beside its reference crop — as #1 | #2, shuffled per item and judge; the key stays
   in `pairkeys/`. Each judge (a FRESH context of the same model, never the builder, never shown another's answer)
   picks per item for SHAPE and, apart, for SURFACE (roughness, wear, colour), and answers the nine 3D-DefectBench
   defects for both. `lune run kit judge <name> --tally` -> win / loss / tie per item, where the judges split, the
   defects a majority saw (`pairwise.json`, and `pairwise` in the verdict). Where B wins, bring that back.
Optimise as you go: the geometry fix names the biggest triangle costs (tiny repeated details: `bevel=0`, fewer
segments; split detail to `group=` if a mesh nears 20k).

**A photo from any angle against the model:**
`lune run kit fit camera <photo> --asset <name>` finds the camera it was taken from
   (silhouette + CMA-ES; the lens from EXIF — a small subject's silhouette cannot show it) ->
   `build/camfit/<name>/overlay.png` (green = both, red = photo only, blue = model only: red / blue that stays is
   where the MODEL differs). Synthetic cameras come back within 0.2 deg when the lens is known (2-3 deg with it
   free); a real 3/4 M107 photo aligns at IoU 0.82 (the rest is the model's own shape). `--ignore <groups>` / `--exclude "x0,y0,x1,y1"` for parts the photo shows
   differently (a deployed bipod); `--points "Muzzle=x,y;Grip=x,y"` pins a thin object's pose by its anchors;
   `--start yaw,pitch,roll` from a known rough view.

## Stage 7 — Present and ship
**Fidelity first:** `rbx.Asset(..., stage="master")` builds the no-limits original — every form as real geometry,
2048 maps, no 20k-per-mesh cap and no triangle budget (still enough triangles to carry its forms). The FIRST product
must look its best; the game version is made from it afterwards. A master never ships as built (`kit models` and
`kit upload` skip it, like `private=True`). On a master, `with a.floaters():` detail is the HIGH-QUALITY version: kept
as real geometry in the group it decorates (seat it on / a hair into the surface — it is checked like every part) and
tagged so the game version bakes it; fine recesses (vent ports, screw holes) are its detail too, never a `tiny_cuts`
warning. **The game version:** `lune run kit optimize <name> [--budget <triangles>]` builds `<name>_game` from the
SAME script: every mesh cut to its share of the tier's budget (1.45x the target: under the over-budget line), the
master's full detail baked onto it (high-to-low: normals, AO, colour), the small coloured details that would smear in
a 1024 map moved to a static `detail` mesh of their own, and the LOSS measured — how far the game surface lies from
the master's (`optimize_loss`: 95% within 0.25% of its length, all within 1.2%) and the reference's silhouette, lines
and look for master and game side by side. A private master's game version stays private. **Presented:**
`lune run kit showcase <name>` renders the last full build under the studio light into build/showcase/<name>/:
poster.png, turntable.gif (it turning once) and mechanism.gif (every joint through its travel in the order the joints
need — `after=` sequences play inside the joint they wait for: the cover opens, THEN the insert lifts out).
`lune run kit upload` → `Models.spawn("crate", {...})` / `Models.tool("rifle", "Rifle")` (PBR maps applied,
anchors become Attachments) · first person: `Viewmodel.new(tool, { offset = ..., arms = true })`.
Uploaded meshes face the right way (the exporter pre-turns them: Roblox's import rotates FBX 180° about Y).
**Someone else's design** (a game's weapon, a film prop — a study or a pipeline test): `rbx.Asset(..., private=True)`.
It builds and is judged like any asset, but `kit models` never puts it in the game and `kit upload` never sends it
(`kit upload --dry-run` lists what would go): keep its reference picture out of git (`refs/private/`, excluded).

## Gates (reference: what every build checks)
Hard: ≤20k tris per mesh · closed mesh · UVs · floating parts · size vs design · inside-out · every a.expect ·
**quality bar** · **fresh review** (hero/aaa) · **stage** (hero/aaa: every traced view at its bar before detail is
judged) · **coverage** (hero/aaa: each hero feature >= 2 px/mm in some picture) · **pbr_values** (hero/aaa) ·
**cad_solid** (every a.cad part a valid, closed solid).
Warnings: buried parts · palette · symmetry · degenerate faces · brief · routes · coverage (props) · pbr_values
(prop) · roughness_variation.
**Deep, per-part (errors at hero/aaa)** — measured, not judged:
- `part_visibility`: ray-cast from 26 directions; `internal()` parts must be hidden, the spec's `visible` is enforced
  (an internal part you can SEE is part of the look: say `through` in the spec and detail it, or close the opening).
- `fastener_support`: pins / screws / rods / rivets must pass through material — a span in open air is reported in mm.
- `clear:<name>`: `a.clear_path("Bore", chamber, muzzle, radius, why=...)` — nothing may block it (bores, sight lines,
  ducts, doorways).
- `spec_parts` / `spec_attach` / `spec_undocumented`: every spec part modelled and touching what it attaches to;
  every model part described ("what IS this in reality?"). Spec = `refs/<name>.spec.json` (`a.spec("<name>")`), or
  inline for a small asset: `a.spec({"parts": [{"name": "Lid", "what": "...", "attaches": ["Body"]}]})`.
- `mechanism`: every joint through its travel, AND no moving part sitting inside another at rest (surface samples,
  5-ray vote, clustered, each part of the other group tested on its own: two parts sharing a face cannot fool it) — a
  barrel through a wall, a trigger inside its housing; the report says AROUND where (studs) and WHICH parts
  ("TopHousing into LaunchTube +97", "Lid in Wall": every face remembers its part through the group join). The travel
  is checked at five stops (a part passing THROUGH something and clear at the end is caught), with any `after=` joints
  posed. A slide needs something to ride on: rails as long as its travel (the spec's `attaches` catches a housing that
  rests on nothing), and every end of a moving part keeps ~1 mm clear of what it passes (flush faces scrape).
- `slivers`: thin shelves on otherwise solid parts (two parallel faces closer than ~1.5 mm) — left when two cuts or
  outlines nearly coincide. Fix at the source: `rbx_trace.region(..., minus=[(piece, gap_mm)], round_mm=2)` splits ONE
  traced shape into pieces with exactly shared seams and filleted outer corners. Parallel means within ~10 degrees: a
  pointed blade (a sword's tip, a feather) thins out to its point by design and is no sliver.
- `plate_support`: a thin flat part (floor plate, armour plate, panel, badge) must rest on its host over >= 70% of its
  back — build it from the host's own edge, not a box. Seated flush or sunk into the host counts as resting
  (`rbx_parts.plate` sinks 0.002 studs, as a mount should).
- `attach_gaps`: a smaller part seated on a bigger one (spec `attaches`) must not show daylight where they face
  (a grip below its seat, a lid not closing); recesses inside a seated interface (rail slots) are fine.
- `marking_placement`: `a.text` must sit on ONE flat face of ONE part — no pins, holes, edges or air under it.
- `projection_*` (warnings, with `a.project` / `a.decal`): `alignment` — the picture's object is not where its trace's
  frame says (said in px: re-trace the picture you paint with); `coverage` — under 80% of what a view sees lands
  inside its outline (the silhouette differs there, or `ignore=` misses a group); `match` — seen from its own camera
  the finished map is not its picture (another layer paints over it, or tex= is too small for its detail; in detail
  mode: its fine structure correlates under 0.3 with the picture's); `detail` — a detail projection moved a
  material's mean colour over 4/255 or transferred no structure (raise `detail_px=`, or the picture has none);
  `decal` — a decal painted nothing (check at= / size= / view).
- `lines_<view>` (its own step after the build, kit/vision/lines.py): one edge detector (TEED) reads the reference
  photo and a shaded render of the model in the same frame; every line INSIDE the outline the photo shows (a receiver
  seam, a magwell plate edge, a row of slots) must have a model line within a few px, and back. Windows along the
  length name where lines are missing / extra; `build/renders/<asset>_lines_<view>.png` = photo / model / diff (red =
  the photo's line the model lacks, blue = extra). hero/aaa FAIL below half the photo's lines; skipped (and said so)
  without the vision models. A reflection or a print that is not geometry: put a `dont_care` box on it.
- `part_review`: every part family gets a tile in `build/renders/parts/<asset>_parts_*.png` (in context — X-ray when
  it is hidden — and isolated) and a line in `assets/models/<asset>.parts.md`:
  `- Part [fp]: real: ... | attaches: ... | works: ... | ref: <picture(s)> | verdict: ok` (or `verdict: fix <what>`
  — fails the build until fixed). `ref:` is REQUIRED: a picture on disk of THIS part on the real object, or of the
  closest SIMILAR part when the object has none (another make's, an exploded diagram's, a patent figure — `kit refs
  patent <n>`); prose is not a reference. `lune run kit refs parts <asset>` lists every part without one and the
  searches to run (the object's part, then a similar object's: the brief's `similar:` line). Inside parts too. The ones to answer NOW are alone on `<asset>_parts_todo.png` (a --geo check renders just those).
  A part whose shape changes (box, volume, area) goes back to TODO with its last answer kept —
  `TODO (changed since: <answer>)`: look again; if it still holds, remove the wrapper, else rewrite it. Answer from
  the references, part by part; this is where "it looks finished" stops being enough.
